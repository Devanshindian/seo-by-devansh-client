Persona — who each article is written to

3–4 reader personas defined once per company. Each article is tagged with one. The persona sets the depth, vocabulary, and angle — not who wrote it.

Concept Output Steps Real example How to pick one

01What a persona is

A persona is the reader the article targets — not the author. It sets the depth, vocabulary, and angle of the piece.

  • Never name the persona in the article — write so readers feel it's for them without being told
  • One article = one persona — if it fits two, pick the one who decides or acts on the topic
Input brand-voice.md — specifically the Audience Understanding section, where the primary and secondary audiences are already captured.

02What this produces

projects/[company]/content-machine/brand-context/persona.md

A short doc: one row per persona (name, who they are, what they read, what they care about, how to pitch to them, what would feel off) plus a rule for how to pick the right persona for a given article.

03Three steps

1

Read brand-voice.md — Audience Understanding section

The raw material is already there. Don't invent personas from scratch.

2

Generate 3–4 distinct reader personas

Run one LLM call with brand-voice.md pasted in. It returns 3–4 non-overlapping personas, each with: name, who they are, what they read, what they care about, how to pitch to them, and what would feel off.

3

Show the user and save

User edits/approves the set, then save to projects/[company]/content-machine/brand-context/persona.md.

04Real example — one persona

From the actual Testlify persona.md:

TA / Recruiting Leader
Who they areHead of TA or Director of Recruiting; owns the hiring function at mid-market to enterprise
ReadsRecruiting metrics, process, tooling, scaling, time-to-hire and cost-per-hire
Cares aboutSpeed + quality at scale, proving ROI, defensible process
Pitch to themLead with the number/benchmark, then the play. Assume they know the basics.

05How to pick one per article

Match the article's topic to the persona who'd actually read it. One article, one persona.
