Style Guide — grammar, punctuation, formatting, SEO

Reads the top 30 blogs and fills a style guide template. 85% is editorial best practice written straight in. The rest — capitalization, punctuation choices, industry terms — comes from reading how the company actually writes.

Concept Output Steps What it covers

01The one rule — three source tags

Every field in the template is tagged by where it comes from. Nothing is invented and no section is left empty.

[BLOGS]

Observed

Decided by reading the real top 30 blogs — headline casing, Oxford comma, quote style, industry terms, preferred words.

[STANDARD]

Best practice

Editorial standard that applies to most companies — written straight into the template, no analysis needed.

[COMPANY]

Confirm with marketing

Filled from what the blogs show, then flagged for the marketing team to confirm — product names, positioning language.

02What this produces

projects/[company]/content-machine/brand-context/style-guide.md

A fully filled style guide — grammar, punctuation, word choice, formatting, SEO standards, brand-specific terms. Every field resolved, no placeholder tags remaining.

Input Top 30 blogs by traffic from content-database.csv (editorial articles only — no product, glossary, or commercial pages).

03Three steps

1

Pull the top 30 blogs

  • Rank all pages in the content database by traffic
  • Keep only editorial blog articles — drop product pages, glossaries, templates, and non-English duplicates
  • Save as a ranked table: Rank · Traffic · Format · Title · URL
2

Analyze — three AI agents read the blogs in parallel

The 30 blogs are split into 3 batches of ~10. Each agent reads its batch and reports only what it actually observes. It looks for:

  • Headline casing — Title Case ("Top 15 Tools") or sentence case ("Top 15 tools")
  • Brand and product naming — how the company name and products are capitalised
  • Industry terms — which terms appear and how they're spelled (e.g. "skills assessment" vs "skills test")
  • Oxford comma — whether the comma appears before "and" in a list ("skills, speed, and accuracy")
  • Em-dash vs hyphen usage — which is preferred and how often
  • Quote style — single or double quotation marks
  • Number style — numerals vs spelled out, how percentages and currency are written
  • Acronyms — which ones appear and need defining on first use
  • Preferred words and words to avoid — consistent choices and hype or filler words that show up

The three agents' findings are merged. For yes/no choices, majority wins. For lists, all findings are combined and deduplicated.

3

Fill the template

  • [STANDARD] fields stay as-is — standard editorial rules that apply to any company
  • [BLOGS] fields are replaced with the matching signal from the Step 2 analysis
  • [COMPANY] fields are filled from what the blogs show, then flagged for marketing to confirm
  • No tag is left unresolved — every placeholder must be replaced before the file ships

04What style-guide.md covers

  • Grammar & mechanics — capitalization (headlines, product names, industry terms), numbers, punctuation, abbreviations and acronyms
  • Word choice — preferred terms (Say This → Not That pairs), words to avoid, inclusive language
  • Formatting — bold/italic use, lists (bulleted vs numbered), links, callout boxes
  • Content structure — intro hook, section length, conclusion and CTA
  • SEO-specific — meta title length, meta description, URL slugs, alt text
  • Dates, stats, images — date format, citing sources, screenshot and chart standards
  • Brand-specific — product and feature naming, how competitors are referenced
  • Voice reminders — a short reminder of the company's voice (full guide lives in brand-voice.md)
  • Editing checklist — every standard check before publishing
