Content Machine · Stage 0 — Brand Context

Writing Examples — show, don't tell

Picks 5 of the company's best real published articles, scores them against brand-voice.md, and assembles each one in full with an annotation on why it's exemplary. The "show" complement to brand-voice.md's "tell."

Concept Output Steps

01The one rule

Every example is a real, published article that genuinely embodies brand-voice.md — pasted in full and annotated with reasons that name specific voice pillars, not generic praise.

  • Every example is a real published article — no paraphrasing, pasted in full
  • Voice fit is scored out of 10 by an AI agent reading each article against brand-voice.md. An article that doesn't match the brand voice gets dropped — even if it's a well-written piece in general.
  • A pillar is a named characteristic from brand-voice.md — e.g. "Confident, Proof-Backed" or "Direct Second-Person Voice." Each annotation reason must name one of those pillars, not just say "good writing."
  • Start with at least 7 candidates so 5 survive after the ones that don't match the voice are cut
Inputs top-pages.csv (the pool, ranked by traffic — primary keyword comes from here, never guessed). brand-voice.md (the scoring yardstick). Saved page files from the brand brain step, or live web-fetch for any not saved.

02What this produces

projects/[company]/content-machine/brand-context/writing-examples.md

5 complete articles pasted verbatim, each with URL, primary keyword, word count, and 2–3 pillar-grounded reasons for why it works. ~13k words total. A writer reads these to understand the voice in action.

03Three steps

1

Classify and select at least 7 articles

  • Start from the Semrush top-pages CSV ranked by traffic
  • Keep only editorial blog articles — drop product pages, glossaries, templates, and tool pages
  • Identify the format of each article from its URL slug alone — no reading required (see format table below)
  • Select at least 7, prioritising high traffic while covering at least 3 different formats
  • Take the primary keyword from the Semrush "Top Keyword" column exactly as-is — never re-derive it from the title

Article formats — how to identify them from the URL

Each format is identified from the URL slug alone, before the article is read. Here is what each format looks like:

URL patternFormatWhat it is
what-are-…, …-definition, types-of-…DefinitionalExplains what something is — "What are pink collar jobs?"
top-15-…, top-8-…, …-questionsListicleA numbered or bulleted list — "60 inventory planner interview questions"
how-to-…, …-process, …-in-5-stepsHow-toStep-by-step instruction — "How to perform a talent review"
…-vs-…, …-alternativesComparisonCompares two things directly — "Job Description vs Job Specification"
…-hiring-process, …-interview-processCase studyHow a named company does something — "Walmart hiring process"
broad opinion or strategy topicThought-leadershipA perspective or argument — "The 4 pillars of skills-based hiring"

This list is not exhaustive — if the site uses a format not listed here, name it plainly and count it. The real Testlify run chose: case study ×2 (Walmart, Netflix), comparison (Job Description vs Job Specification), definitional (Halo and Horn Effect), listicle (25 best talent assessment tools).

2

Score and annotate each article

  • Each article gets its own AI agent that reads it alongside brand-voice.md and scores it out of 10
  • The score is based on how well it matches the brand voice — not general writing quality
  • Articles that score poorly are dropped with no annotation
  • Articles that pass get 2–3 annotation reasons, each naming a specific pillar from brand-voice.md — not just "this is well-written"
  • If fewer than 5 pass, pull another batch from Step 1 targeting the formats that scored well. Older articles often fail — this is expected. Keep going until 5 survive.
  • Each passing article is assembled: metadata from the Semrush table + annotation + full article text pasted word-for-word
3

Quality gate

  • Exactly 5 examples — not fewer
  • Each has all fields: URL, title, primary keyword, word count, annotation, full verbatim text
  • The set covers at least 3 different formats
  • Every annotation reason names a specific pillar — no generic praise
  • Full article pasted word-for-word — no snippets
