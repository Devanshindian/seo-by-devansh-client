SEO / AEO / GEO — the per-article checklist

The single gate every article passes before it publishes. It is not a scripted engine — it is the standard the writer verifies each draft against. Research hands the writer a keyword-set and a brief; the writer drafts to this checklist and ticks every box; the article ships only when it scores 80+ with no hard failures. This page explains each rule, the three engines it serves, and the worked examples behind the tricky lines.

01The one idea

One article, written once, has to win in three places at once: the blue links, the answer box, and the AI chat reply. The good news is they mostly reward the same thing — a genuinely helpful, well-structured, well-sourced page. Write well for one and you largely cover all three.

🔎

SEO

Search Engine Optimisation — rank in Google's list of blue links. Won with backlinks, trust signals (E-E-A-T), fast pages, and depth on the topic.

📦

AEO

Answer Engine Optimisation — be the boxed answer at the top of Google (the featured snippet) and the spoken voice answer. Won with one clean, liftable 40–60 word answer per question.

🤖

GEO

Generative Engine Optimisation — get quoted and cited by ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Claude when they write an answer. Won with in-body stats, quotes, and outbound citations to named primary sources.

The one rule that governs everything SEO serves the reader, never the algorithm. The best SEO is content that is genuinely the most helpful page on the topic. Every rule below is a way of making a genuinely helpful page legible to a machine — not a trick that games one. An article publishes only when it clears the gate: Overall score ≥ 80 and zero P0 (hard-fail) checks.

02The words you need first

The jargon the checklist leans on, defined once in plain language. Everything after this reuses these exact terms.

SERP — Search Engine Results Page. The page of results Google returns for a query, including the blue links plus the boxed features around them.
Featured snippet — the boxed answer Google lifts to the very top of the SERP (often called "position zero"). It is pulled verbatim from a page, so the goal is to write a passage clean enough to be lifted whole.
PAA (People Also Ask) — the expandable list of related questions on the SERP. These are harvested during research and become the article's FAQ and section-lead answers.
Liftable answer — a 40–60 word, self-contained answer placed immediately under a heading, written so an answer engine or AI can quote it out of context and it still makes sense. The core AEO move.
TL;DR / Key Takeaways — a 4–6 bullet summary placed above the fold, right after the intro. A reader who reads only the TL;DR should grasp the argument. It is a summary, not a stat dump.
E-E-A-T — Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness. Google's quality signals: the markers that show a real, credentialed person who knows the topic wrote the page.
Canonicalise — when two pages overlap, point the duplicate at one master URL so they stop competing with each other in search.
rel policy — the link attribute that tells engines how to treat an outbound link: internal links = dofollow (pass authority), editorial citations = nofollow noopener, paid/partner = sponsored, user-generated = nofollow. Never ship a paid link as dofollow.
Reverse-silo — an internal link that points from a blog post up to a product or feature page, so topical articles funnel authority (and readers) toward what the company sells.
P0 — a hard-fail check. If a P0 fails, the article is blocked from publishing regardless of how high it scores. The non-negotiable list is in §08.

03The three engines & the five levers

Why the checklist looks the way it does. SEO, AEO and GEO are three different machines reading the same page for different things. The five levers are where effort compounds — spend it top-down, lever 1 first and densely.

The five levers that compound

#LeverWhy it matters
1Inline stats + direct quotes + outbound citations to named primary sourcesThe strongest causal lift on AI citation. The Princeton GEO study found each of these adds roughly +30–40% citation likelihood.
2E-E-A-T on the pageGoogle's trust gate, and AI weighs source authority. The author box is rendered natively by the theme from the author field — never written into the body.
3Direct 40–60 word answers under question H2sWhat makes a section eligible for the featured snippet and the AI Overview. One clean liftable answer per section.
4Topical authority3+ internal links to related posts, varied anchors (under 30% exact-match), and semantic coverage of the whole topic — not just the exact keyword.
5Freshness + substantive changedateModified current, current-year framing where natural, post-2022 sources. It must be a real update, not a date bump.
Don't rely on these to rank Some things give little-to-no independent ranking lift. We still build the harmless ones — they cost almost nothing and aid parsing/UX — we just don't count on them:
  • FAQ / HowTo / Speakable schema: no rich result for general sites since Aug 2023. Build it anyway; expect no ranking lift.
  • Schema for AI citations: roughly zero independent lift. Citations come from in-body stats/quotes/sources (lever 1), not markup.
  • Keyword stuffing, exact-match anchor spam, density-% targets: actively penalised — genuinely avoid.
  • "Longer ranks higher": false. Hit the target band for the type; density beats volume.

The links, the answer-engine mechanics, and the integrity of every claim. This is where the GEO lift and the hard-fail checks live.

Links4 checks
≥3 internal links, well-anchored:
  • varied, descriptive anchor text (under 30% exact-match)
  • never "click here" / "read more"
  • ≤2 links per paragraph, distributed not clustered
One internal link to a product / feature page where natural (reverse-silo)
≥2 external links to credible primary sources · rel policy applied (internal dofollow · citation nofollow · paid sponsored)
No competitor cited auto-enforced upstream · every link returns 200
AEO / GEO3 checks
Direct 40–60 word answer under question H2s · for question-led formats ≥50% of H2s are questions · 4+ inline citations
Built to be quoted by AI:
  • quotable, self-contained sentences (precise numbers, dates, entities)
  • one idea per section
  • ≥1 embedded video where it adds context
≥1 original / proprietary stat bonus, not a gate
Sources & claims integrity4 checks
Every stat traces to its exact source: P0

Cite the most credible + freshest source available (model-decided; no fixed whitelist)
Self-consistency: every number reconciles (no impossible math)

The liftable answer — what a 40–60 word answer looks like

Under a question H2 H2: What is a skills assessment?
A skills assessment is a structured test that measures a candidate's ability to do specific job tasks, rather than what their résumé claims. It scores real work — coding, writing, reasoning, or role-specific scenarios — so hiring teams compare people on evidence, not gut feel. (≈45 words: self-contained, precise, quotable out of context.)

08Score & ship

The final gate. Score the draft, clear the hard-fails, run the AI-writing pass, then publish — or ship as a labelled draft and report it.

The scoring rubric

Score each post 0–100 across five weighted dimensions. Publish when the overall clears 80.

Overall = SEO ×0.25 + Structure ×0.15 + Content ×0.25 + EEAT ×0.15 + AEO/GEO ×0.20  →  publish when ≥ 80

A P0 failure caps the overall below the threshold until it is fixed. Rework the lowest-scoring items and re-score — cap 3 passes. If it still can't reach 80 without fabricating or padding, ship it as a draft and report it.
