Writing Integrity — the honest writing contract

The rules for making truthful, well-sourced, distinctive claims. This is read by the writer before and during drafting. Applied at write time — before shipping.

Authority honesty No overpromise No hype Differentiation Self-consistency Citations Stats

01Authority honesty — claim only what the product actually is

Position the product only where it genuinely wins. Nowhere else.

  • When the topic is broad, write the broad context honestly, then locate the product at its real slice
  • Claiming one slice well beats claiming the whole stack vaguely
  • If the product isn't the answer for a use case, say so plainly — it builds trust

02No overpromise; acknowledge limitations

Never guarantee outcomes. Never claim "this will fix hiring."

  • Acknowledge trade-offs and limits explicitly
  • Transparent always beats salesy

03No empty hype or unprovable superlatives

Every strong claim carries a real, cited number. Otherwise cut it.

  • Ban hype words with no proof: "ultimate", "game-changing", "revolutionary", "seamless", "world-class"
  • Ban buzzword soup: "synergistic", "holistically", "paradigm"
  • If you can't cite it, don't write it

04Differentiation — say something only we'd say

Carry a real point of view, a non-obvious trade-off, or a concrete workflow — not generic vendor filler.

  • Clearly-hypothetical scenarios are allowed ("a 500-person SaaS hiring 20 engineers a quarter could…") if tied to a genuine product capability
  • HARD BAN: no named or implied real customer, no fake quote, no fabricated first-party metric (win rate, time-to-hire delta, NPS, customer count)
  • Use real first-party data ONLY if it was actually supplied for this run
Examples of what NOT to do "One of our customers cut time-to-hire by 60%" — unless you have that customer's written consent and verified data. "Our platform boasts a 94% adoption rate" — unless DataForSEO or your internal data actually says this for this run. "I once worked with a team that…" in a company byline — company bylines use collective markers only, never fabricated personal stories.

05Self-consistency — the numbers must reconcile

List every number used. Confirm none contradict each other.

  • An adoption %, an ROI figure, and a time-to-hire must all be possible together
  • Fix conflicts: re-verify the stat or drop it
  • Never ship with unresolved contradictions

06Citation integrity — cite, don't promote

A citation is evidence for one stat. Never write "X has a great guide on this." That's promotion.

  • State the fact, cite the source (neutral, nofollow), move on
  • Max 2 citations per external domain — aim for 3+ distinct domains
  • Prefer the most credible and most recent source available — the model judges credibility; there's no fixed whitelist
  • Never cite a direct competitor as a source — use a neutral primary instead

07No stat quota — use stats where they earn their place

Don't force a fixed number of statistics. Don't stuff.

  • Use a stat where it genuinely supports the point
  • A few load-bearing verified stats beat many impressive-sounding ones
  • Natural over volume
