Content Machine · Research Phase

How research-structure turns the research into the article's blueprint

This is the last step of the research phase. It takes everything gathered for one topic — STORM's evidence and our own site pages — and turns it into the article's blueprint: a menu of candidate sections where each one already carries its evidence and its links. The write phase then picks from that menu. Here is exactly how it is built, script by script.

01The one idea

The problem: STORM hands over tens of thousands of words of evidence with no structure. This engine builds the structure — and it builds it around one argument, deciding what the article says before deciding how anything is grouped or named.

🧩

1. Gather

Break every source into small cards, one idea each, each keeping the sentence it came from and its link.

✂️

2. Prune

Score every card against what this article argues and drop the off-spine tail before it can bloat the structure.

🗂️

3. Organize

Group the survivors into non-overlapping sections, each named from its own cards, each card in exactly one place.

🔗

4. Assemble

Hang the evidence and links back on each section by ID, add the FAQ and the reading order, and render the blueprint.

The one rule that governs everything
Every section is built from cards, and every card lands in exactly one section. Nothing in the blueprint is written from the model's own knowledge: if a line cannot be traced back to a harvested card with a source, it does not belong. The "exactly one" half is not a hope, it is asserted in code.
Why this engine no longer buys keywords
It used to buy a search keyword for every section it designed. Those sections are a research menu, and the write phase redesigns them into the real article, so the money went on headings that stopped existing. One live run bought 5 keywords for 17 sections and 3 of them were for sections the spine filter now deletes outright. Keyword buying moved to the architect, where the sections are final.

02The words you need first

A small vocabulary that everything else leans on. Here it is in plain language, once.

Blueprint — the output: a menu of candidate sections (H2 → H3) where each one already holds its evidence and its links, plus the FAQ and the keyword set. The write phase picks from it.
Card — the unit of work: one atomic idea from one source, with a one-line summary, the exact source text, and its links. Everything downstream operates on cards, not documents.
Gloss — a card's one-line summary. Deliberately the only thing the grouping step reads, which keeps the call small and stops the evidence text from swamping the decision.
Verbatim — the exact source sentences, copied not reworded, kept so the claim can be checked later.
Tag — where the card came from: storm (deep research) or ownpage (one of our own site pages).
Spine — one short paragraph saying what this article argues, for whom, and what the reader can do at the end. Written earlier in the research phase, before any card existed. It is the single test the prune step applies.
The world — two lines, about and not about, written at the very start of the research phase. They mark the boundary of the subject, so material from a neighbouring field that shares our words can be spotted and cut.
Persona — the one reader this article serves, chosen from the brand's persona table. Picked once here and reused, so research and writing aim at the same person.
MECE — "mutually exclusive, collectively exhaustive": every card sits in exactly one section, nothing missed and nothing repeated. Guaranteed in code.
Orphan keyword — a high-demand keyword that no section happens to cover. A safety-net flag, so building from content does not quietly miss obvious search demand.

03The card — the atom everything is built from

Before anything can be organised, every source is shredded into cards. A card is one idea from one place. Making the card the atom is what lets the engine prune, group and cite with precision.

id
A single continuous number across every source (STORM cards 1…N, then our own pages from N+1). A code check makes sure none is skipped.
gloss
The one-line summary. The only field the grouping step reads.
verbatim
The exact source text, kept so the claim can be verified.
source_urls
Where the verbatim came from. Becomes the section's external citations, and is shown to the prune step, because where a subject shares a word with another field the source is often the only thing that gives it away.
internal_link
Set when the card is one of our pages. Becomes an internal link.
tag
storm ownpage — which of the two sources it came from.

How each source becomes cards

The gloss always reads the whole chunk it summarises, never just a heading. What is written by the model and what is lifted by code differs between the two sources, deliberately, so citations stay faithful:

SourceGlossVerbatimTag
STORM chunkwritten by the model from the whole chunkthe chunk text, keeping its citation markers, and checked to exist in the sourcestorm
Our own pagewritten by the model from the section textsliced by code from the page body, so it is faithful by construction and never model-copiedownpage
The anti-fabrication check
STORM is the only source whose verbatim the model writes, so each STORM verbatim is checked as an exact substring of its source section. If it is not really there, the card is dropped. Own-page verbatim is sliced by code straight from the page. Nothing gets cited that was not actually said.
Two sources, not three
Competitor headings, gaps and search questions used to be harvested into cards here as well, from the keyword research brief. They no longer are, for one reason: those cards carried no evidence behind them, only a phrase, and the write phase reads the same three lists directly from the keyword-research files anyway. Laundering them through clustering made a bare phrase look like researched material.

04The architecture — free judgment, guaranteed code

The whole engine is free. Every judgment runs on a headless command-line model (Claude or Codex, no key), and everything that must be identical on every run is plain code.

🧠

The model judges

where a formula would get it wrong
It decides
  • Which reader persona this article serves
  • Whether each card serves the spine, and whether it is protected
  • How the surviving cards group into themes
  • What each theme is called and how it splits into sub-topics
  • The reading order of the sections
⚙️

Code guarantees

where drift is unacceptable
It enforces
  • Every card is placed exactly once, asserted after grouping
  • The reading order is a genuine permutation, nothing added or lost
  • Evidence and links are re-attached by card ID, pure assembly
  • The FAQ is lifted straight from the search results, no model involved
  • The keyword set is read from JSON, no model involved
Nothing here spends money any more
The one paid step, a keyword lookup per section, was removed. Every remaining call runs on the local command-line model. The engine still reads the keyword-research files that were paid for earlier in the phase, but it buys nothing of its own.

05The full pipeline — from sources to blueprint

One command runs it all, through four phases: gather → prune → organize → finish. Each step writes a named output the next step reads. code marks a guarantee done in plain code.

Phase A · Gather
1

Harvest cards free

Read every source in its own small call, so nothing gets truncated, and emit cards with continuous IDs.

  • harvest_storm.py — splits each STORM dossier (the hub plus every gap-fill iteration) by section, one call per section, and resolves each citation marker back to a real URL.
  • harvest_ownpages.py — reads each relevant page's body from the site catalogue, keeps the real content sections and skips navigation, footer and calls to action, then slices the verbatim by code.
  • cards.json — every card, numbered continuously. A section that yields zero cards is retried and then flagged loudly, never silently dropped.

1a

Pick the reader persona free

Choose the one reader this article serves from the brand's persona table. Decided once, here, and reused, so the research and the writing target the same person.

  • pick_persona.py → persona.json. Falls back to a generic "practitioner, not academic" reader when no persona table exists.
  • personas.py — reads the brand's persona table from the company's own brand-context folder, so a new company only needs its own file and nothing here changes.
Spokes build even when they are not in the topic sheet
harvest_ownpages.py pulls a page's internal-link candidates from the clubbed-ideas sheet. A newly minted spoke has no row there. It used to exit with "asset not found" and kill the whole build; now it warns and returns an empty list:
  • Own pages from that sheet are enrichment, internal-link candidates, not a hard input.
  • The real content is the STORM cards, so no own pages means fewer internal links, not a dead build.

  • score_cards.py → dropped-cards.json, with every drop logged with its score and reason.

4

Name and split each theme free

One call per theme, in a small context, writes its working section heading and, when it is big, splits its cards across sub-topics. These headings are working drafts; the final wording is the architect's job.

  • name_clusters.py → sections.json. Code guarantees every member card lands under the section or exactly one of its sub-topics.

Under each section, collect its cards' verbatim and source URLs as external links, and our own page URLs as internal links, kept as separate lists. Pure assembly by card ID: nothing new is written here.

Phase D · Finish
7

Orphan check free

Scan the keyword pool already bought earlier in the phase, with no new lookups, for any high-demand keyword no section covers. The safety net that stops a content-first build from missing obvious demand.

  • orphan.py — flags orphans, and adds a candidate section when one is worth it.
8

FAQ and reading order free code

The FAQ is taken straight from the "People Also Ask" questions in the saved search results and deduplicated, in code, with no model involved. The order is one call that sequences the sections into a logical flow, then code checks the result is a real permutation.

  • faq_order.py — the FAQ list plus the sections reordered.
8b

Keyword set code

Collect the keywords the article should cover: the primary, its variations, the secondaries and the in-body list. Pure code — it opens the keyword-research JSON and flattens it.

  • keyword_set.py — this used to pay a model to read the same numbers back out of rendered markdown, a round trip that could only ever drift from its own source.
9

Render the blueprint free

Assemble everything into the machine-readable source of truth and a readable viewer.

  • render.py → structure-<slug>.json and structure-<slug>.html, with a contents sidebar, hover an evidence point to see its source links, and clickable citation numbers that jump to a references list.

06The spine filter — the biggest lever on quality

STORM is thorough, which means it floods the grouping step with material that is genuinely well sourced and genuinely about something else. Left in, that becomes encyclopedia-scale sections. So before grouping, every card is scored and the off-spine tail is dropped, but never the load-bearing data.

🎯

One test, at the card level

Each card is scored on a single question: does this serve the spine? Judging the atomic card rather than the section means a good statistic buried inside a bad section survives on its own merit.

🛡️

The PROTECT rule

A card is never dropped, whatever its score, when it carries hard data: a number, a percentage, a coefficient, a threshold, a statistic, a sample item, or a link between a named option and an outcome.

🪜

Tunable, conservative first

A card is dropped only when its relevance is at or below the threshold and it is unprotected. Start conservative, read the drops, then tighten. A drop rate above the flag level raises a loud warning.

The bug this rewrite fixed
The judge used to score "usefulness to the brand's reader". That is a question a well-sourced card about a neighbouring topic answers perfectly, so it scored highly and sailed through. It now judges one thing only — does this serve the spine — and it sees three extra pieces of context that make that answerable:
  • The spine, and the two world lines, so the boundary of the subject is explicit.
  • All four brand personas, with the one picked in step 1a named as the primary reader.
  • Each card's source. Where a subject shares a word with another field, the source is often the only thing that reveals it: a university page about a student prize contest, a clinical use of the same term.
Why it must be card-level, not section-level
Reading the real cards proved that good data hides inside off-topic sections. A "80%+ of the Fortune 500 use tests" statistic sat inside a history section, and reliability thresholds sat next to academic theory. A section-level pruner kills those. Scoring the atomic card and protecting hard data keeps them.

What it did on a real run (the hiring-hackathon topic)

786
cards after harvest
→
373
kept (413 dropped, 52.5%)

Every card was scored, none went unscored, and the run stayed under the 60% warning level. The 373 survivors became 12 sections. A sample drop reads: "about the assignment brief's fairness and legal scope, not the judging rubric or AI-use policy" — the kind of card that is real, well sourced, and about a different article. Deletion happens before grouping, so nothing downstream can dangle, and because harvest is exhaustive the same fact usually sits on several cards, so dropping one rarely loses it.

07MECE clustering — one home per idea, guaranteed

Now the surviving cards are grouped into the article's themes. Three design choices make this reliable: the grouping sees the article, it reads only the gloss, and "one card, one section" is proven in code.

🏷️

Group for THIS article

the model step
It sees the article, and only the gloss
The call receives the title, the angle, the spine, the two world lines and the four personas, so it groups for this article instead of grouping blind. Blind grouping is what laundered off-spine material into tidy-looking chapters: a well-named cluster makes off-topic cards look like a legitimate section. It still reads only the ID and the one-line gloss, so the heavy source text never enters the decision, and evidence is re-attached by ID afterwards.
✅

MECE verified in code

the code step, not the model
A guarantee, not a hope
Code keeps each card in its first cluster only, gives any unplaced cards one more pass, drops leftovers into an explicit "Unsorted (review)" bucket, then asserts that every card is placed exactly once. There is no "model, please check your own work" loop.
Big sets split in two levels
Above a threshold in config.py, the cards are batched, each batch is clustered in parallel, and the batch labels are then merged into final themes. Every call stays small, so nothing times out, and the merge step sees the article context too.
Why non-overlap is the whole game
Two sections can only fight over the same search phrase when they cover the same thing. Making the sections non-overlapping kills that at the root, which is what later makes it safe for the architect to research one keyword per section. Solve the overlap structurally and per-section keywords stop being risky.

08What comes out, and where it goes

Two files: the machine source of truth the write phase consumes, and a page a human can read.

What the blueprint contains

  • Ordered sections, each with its sub-topics, carrying its label, its evidence, and its internal and external links.
  • An FAQ taken from the real "People Also Ask" questions for this topic.
  • The keyword set the article should cover: the primary, its variations, the secondaries, the in-body list.
  • Orphan flags, for any high-demand keyword no section covers.
  • Write guidance — the SEO shape rules, carried alongside for the writer to apply rather than pre-applied here.

The viewer (HTML)

A research menu, not the final article
This produces the organised menu of possible sections, and it is bigger than the article on purpose:
  • The research phase stays exhaustive — capture everything the topic could cover.
  • The write phase trims to the sections that actually fit the article, and does the final wording.
  • The menu carries no introduction, summary or close. Those are added much later, by the writer.
On a real run the hiring-hackathon topic rendered 12 sections and the interview-questions topic 37, from the same pipeline. The spread is the point: the menu is as big as the material honestly is.
Where research-structure sits in the bigger machine
This is the last step of the research phase, the pivot that turns everything gathered into the article's skeleton. It reads STORM's evidence and our own pages, and hands the write phase a blueprint complete enough that the writer never has to hunt for a fact, a link or a structure. It runs once per article: a pillar (flat output), or a spoke nested under its pillar with --hub. Everything to do with search keywords now happens either before it, in the keyword research, or after it, in the architect.
