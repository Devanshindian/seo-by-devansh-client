Content Machine · Research Phase

How research-structure turns the research into the article's blueprint

This is the last step of the research phase. It takes everything gathered for one topic — STORM's evidence, our own pages, the DataForSEO brief — and turns it into the article's blueprint: a skeleton of sections where each one already carries its evidence, its links, and its target keyword. The writer then just writes. Here is exactly how it's built, script by script.

01The one idea

The problem: STORM gives us tens of thousands of words of evidence, but not a structure. This engine builds the structure — and it does it content-first: shape the sections from the richest material, then tag keywords on afterwards. Never the other way round.

🧩

1. Gather

Break every source — STORM, our pages, the brief — into small cards, one idea each.

✂️

2. Prune

Score each card through the reader's eyes and drop the off-angle tail before it bloats the structure.

🗂️

3. Organize

Group the surviving cards into non-overlapping sections (H2/H3), each named from its own cards.

🔗

4. Enrich

Hang evidence, links, and a target keyword on each section, then render the blueprint.

The one rule that governs everything
Content-first, then keywords — and every card lands in exactly one section. The skeleton is built from the rich content (evidence + competitor coverage + gaps + our pages); keywords are tags on sections, never the skeleton. If a section's content can't be traced back to a harvested card, it doesn't belong.
Why content-first, not keyword-first
If keywords seeded the sections, thin keywords would give a thin article — and our keyword list is often thin on the exact angle. So we build from the rich evidence we actually gathered, and map keywords on after. Google rewards complete, helpful content, not keyword-shaped content.

02The words you need first

This engine has a small vocabulary that everything else leans on. Here it is in plain language, once.

Blueprint — the output: the article's skeleton (H1 → H2 → H3 → FAQ) where every section already holds its evidence, its links, and its target keyword. The writer consumes this and just writes.
Card — the unit of work: one atomic idea from one source, with a one-line summary, the exact source text, its links, and a tag. Everything downstream operates on cards, not documents.
Gloss — a card's one-line summary. Deliberately the only thing the grouping step reads (keeps it fast and stops the evidence text from bloating the decision).
Verbatim — the exact source sentence(s), copied not reworded, kept for citation.
Tag — what kind of card it is: storm ownpage competitor gap question.
Persona — the one reader this article serves. Chosen once here and reused later, so the research and the writing aim at the same person.
MECE — "mutually exclusive, collectively exhaustive": every card sits in exactly one section — nothing missed, nothing repeated. Guaranteed in code, not hoped for.
Differentiator — a section that carries our unique angle, or a gap competitors don't cover. The few sections where we actually beat the field.
Orphan keyword — a high-demand keyword that no section happens to cover; a safety-net flag so content-first doesn't miss obvious search demand.

03The card — the atom everything is built from

Before anything can be organized, every source is shredded into cards. A card is one idea from one place. Making the card the atom is what lets the engine prune, cluster, and cite with precision.

id
A single continuous number across every source (STORM cards 1…N, then our first page N+1…). A code check makes sure none is skipped.
gloss
The one-line summary. The only field the clustering step reads.
verbatim
The exact source text (or empty for cards that have no underlying text, like a competitor heading).
source_urls
Where the verbatim came from — becomes the section's external citations.
internal_link
Set when the card is one of our pages — becomes an internal link.
tag
storm ownpage competitor gap question — where the card came from and how it's treated.

How each source becomes cards

The gloss always reads the whole chunk it summarises, never just a heading. What's LLM-written vs lifted differs by source — and that's deliberate, so citations stay faithful:

SourceGlossVerbatimTag
STORM chunkLLM-written from the whole chunkthe chunk text (keeps its citation markers); checked to exist in the sourcestorm
Our own pageLLM-written from the section textcode-sliced from the page — 100% faithful, never LLM-copiedownpage
Competitor H2 (from the brief)the heading itself— (none)competitor
Gap we can own (from the brief)the gap phrase itself— (none)gap
PAA / AI question (from the brief)the question itself— (none)question
The anti-fabrication check
STORM is the only source whose verbatim the LLM writes, so each STORM verbatim is checked as an exact substring of its source section — if it isn't really there, the card is dropped. Own-page verbatim is sliced by code straight from the page, so it's faithful by construction. Nothing gets cited that wasn't actually said.

04The architecture — free judgment, one paid lookup

Nearly the whole engine is free thinking on a headless LLM CLI (Claude or Codex), plus some pure code for the parts that must be guaranteed (like MECE). Only one step spends money, and it's skippable.

05The full pipeline — from sources to blueprint

One command runs it all. It moves through five phases: gather → prune → organize → enrich → finish. Each step writes a named output the next step reads. code = a guarantee done in plain code; paid = the one DataForSEO step.

Phase A · Gather
1

Harvest cards free

Read every source in its own small call (so nothing truncates) and emit cards with continuous ids.

  • harvest_storm.py — splits each STORM dossier (hub + every gap-fill iteration) by section, one call per section; resolves each citation marker to a real URL.
  • harvest_ownpages.py — fetches our relevant pages live, keeps the real content sections (skips nav/footer/CTA), slices the verbatim by code.
  • harvest_brief.py — lifts competitor H2s, gaps-we-can-own, and questions straight from the brief.
  • cards.json — every card, numbered continuously. A 0-card section is retried, then flagged loudly — never silently dropped.

2·3

Group the surviving cards into non-overlapping sections reading only the gloss (anti-bloat), then a code pass guarantees every card sits in exactly one section.

4

Name + split each section free

One call per cluster (small context) writes its working H2 label and, if it's big, splits its cards across H3s. Labels are working drafts — final wording is the writer's job.

  • name_clusters.py → sections.json.
4c

Re-decide the differentiator flag free

The "this is our angle" flag set during clustering over-fires (it never saw the angle or the competitors). This step re-decides it once, strictly — given the asset's angle + what competitors cover + our gaps.

  • redecide_differentiator.py → differentiator-audit.json (the authoritative flag; supersedes clustering's guess).
Phase D · Enrich
4b

Strip competitor sources code

Before evidence is attached, drop every competitor citation so none survives into the blueprint. A card sourced only from a competitor is deleted; a mixed card keeps its legit source, competitor link removed.

  • filter_sources.py + competitors.py → competitor-drops.json. Flags loudly if it deletes more than 5% of cards.
5

Attach evidence + links code

Under each section, collect its cards' verbatim + source URLs (external links) and our-page links (internal), kept as separate lists. Pure assembly by card id — nothing new is written.

  • attach.py — folds evidence + links into each section.
6

Target keyword per H2 paid · DataForSEO

For each section: an LLM makes 1–3 seed phrases → DataForSEO returns real phrases containing them → keep only KD < 30 and volume ≥ 100 → an LLM picks the best fit. Differentiator/gap sections with no qualifying keyword are left keyword-free — that's correct.

  • keywords.py — a keyword + KD + volume on each H2. Skip with --skip-keywords.

7

Orphan check free

Scan the brief's existing keyword pool (no new API calls) for any high-demand keyword no section covers — the safety net that stops content-first from missing obvious demand.

  • orphan.py — flags orphans, adds a candidate section if one's worth it.
Phase E · Finish
8

FAQ + order free code

Build the FAQ from the question cards (code, no LLM), then order the sections using the AI Overview grouping from the brief (logical order if there's none).

  • faq_order.py — the FAQ list + the sections reordered.
8b

Keyword-set free

Consolidate the keywords the writer must cover, from two sources: top-down from the brief (primary + variations + secondaries + in-body), and bottom-up from the sections (each H2's own keyword).

Assemble everything into the machine-readable source of truth and an interactive viewer.

  • render.py → structure-<slug>.json (the blueprint) + structure-<slug>.html (hover an H2 → its keyword/KD/volume; hover evidence → its source links).

06The angle-relevance filter — the biggest lever on quality

Rich STORM floods the clusterer with off-angle cards — field history, off-topic uses, deep academic theory. Left in, they become encyclopedia-scale sections. So before clustering, every card is scored and the off-angle tail is dropped — but never the load-bearing data.

🎯

Score at the card level

Each card is scored 0–5 on how directly it helps the reader choose / evaluate / interpret / use the thing we offer. Judging the atomic card (not the section) means a good stat buried in a bad section survives on its own merit.

🛡️

The PROTECT rule

A card is never dropped, whatever its score, if it's tagged gap/competitor, or carries hard data — a number, %, coefficient, threshold, stat, sample, or ties an option to an outcome.

🪜

Tunable, conservative first

Drop a card only if relevance is at or below the threshold (default 1) and it's unprotected. Start conservative, read the drops, then tighten. An implausibly high drop rate raises a loud flag.

Why it must be card-level, not section-level
Reading the real cards proved good data hides inside off-angle sections: an "80%+ of Fortune 500 use tests" stat sat inside a history section; reliability thresholds sat beside academic validity theory. A section-level pruner would kill those. Scoring the atomic card + the PROTECT rule keeps them.

What it did on a real run (the psychometric-tests topic)

1,227
cards after harvest
→
715
cards kept (512 dropped, 41.7%)

Off-angle sections were gutted (field history ~97% dropped, deep theory ~81%), while 14 of 14 must-survive cards were kept and nothing scoring ≥2 was dropped. Downstream that took H3 sections 135 → 107 and evidence items 1,188 → 678. Deletion happens before clustering, so nothing can dangle — and since harvest is exhaustive, the same fact usually sits on several cards, so dropping one rarely loses it.

07MECE clustering — one home per idea, guaranteed

Now the surviving cards are grouped into the article's sections. Two design choices make this reliable: the grouping reads only the gloss, and "one card, one section" is proven in code.

🏷️

Cluster on the gloss only

the LLM step
Sees just id + one-line gloss
The heavy verbatim never enters the grouping call, so the decision stays fast and un-bloated. Evidence is re-attached by id afterwards. Cards with no verbatim (gaps, questions, competitor headings) cluster on their gloss too — they contribute what to cover; the evidence comes from the STORM/own-page cards beside them.
✅

MECE verified in code

the code step — not the LLM
A guarantee, not a hope
Code keeps each card in its first cluster only, gives any unplaced cards one more pass, drops leftovers into an explicit "Unsorted (review)" bucket, then asserts every card is placed exactly once. No "LLM, please review yourself" loop.
Why non-overlap is the whole game
Two sections can only fight over the same keyword when they cover the same thing. Making the sections non-overlapping kills that at the root — which is exactly what makes it safe to map one target keyword per section in Step 6. Solve the overlap structurally, and per-section keywords become safe instead of risky.

08Keywords last, and the blueprint that comes out

Only now — with clean, non-overlapping sections — do keywords get mapped on. Then everything renders into the blueprint the writer consumes.

🔑

One keyword per section

A fresh DataForSEO lookup per section keeps only KD < 30, volume ≥ 100, then picks the best fit. Safe because sections don't overlap.

🚫

Don't force keywords

A differentiator or gap section with no qualifying keyword is left keyword-free. Some of our best sections have no search volume — and that's correct. Forcing one recreates the thin-keyword problem.

🕳️

Orphan safety-net

Any high-demand keyword no section covers is flagged (and a candidate section added if it's worth one) — so content-first doesn't miss real demand.

What the blueprint contains

  • H1 → ordered H2s → H3s, each section carrying its label, its evidence, its internal + external links, and its target keyword (KD + volume).
  • An FAQ built from the question cards.
  • The keyword-set the writer must cover, and write-guidance — the SEO shape rules (≤60-char H1, 2–3 keyword-carrying H2s, ≥50% questions, answer-first) for the writer to apply.
🗂️

The blueprint (JSON)

The machine source of truth the write phase consumes — every section with its evidence, links, and keyword.

structure-<slug>.json
👀

The interactive viewer (HTML)

Hover a section to see its keyword/KD/volume; hover evidence to see its source links. For reading and review.

structure-<slug>.html
It's a menu, not the final article
This produces the complete, organized menu of possible sections — it is not trimmed to the final 4–7 H2s and carries no intro, TL;DR, or close. The write phase selects which sections ship and does the wording; the SEO shape travels alongside as guidance for the writer to apply, not something pre-applied here.
