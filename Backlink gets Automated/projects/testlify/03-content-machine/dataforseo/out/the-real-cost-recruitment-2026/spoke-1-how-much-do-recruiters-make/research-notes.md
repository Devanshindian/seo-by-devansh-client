# Research notes — Recruiter Per-Hire Pay Commission Breakdown (agent commentary)

*Separate from the clean brief. What happened, calls made, what's weak, follow-ups.*

## Judgment calls
- Primary is `how much do recruiters make` (1600/KD6), not the asset's own title phrase `how much do recruiters make per hire` (260/KD0) — the plural, cross-type phrasing matches the angle's agency/retained/RPO/in-house breakdown better than the narrower per-hire wording, so the exact-title phrase was kept as an H1/variation instead of forced into the tracked primary slot. This is a deliberate volume-over-verbatim-match call.
- Five near-duplicate "how much does/can a recruiter make/earn" rewords (1600/1600/1600/1600 vol) were folded into variations rather than each anchoring its own section — same intent as the primary, no incremental angle value.
- `trykondo.com` occupied two SERP slots (rank 3 and 9) for the same URL — treated as one competitor, not two, so the "10 results" competitive count is really 9 distinct pages.
- Two in-body-only concepts (RPO recruiter fees, contingency recruiter fee percentage) got no anchoring keyword despite being core to the angle — kept in-body because they're structurally required by the "commission rates by type" promise in the asset title, not because a keyword justified them. Flagging this as a deliberate protect-style inclusion, not a metric-driven one.
- One PAA/related-search item (military recruiter per-hire pay, vol 110) was routed to spoke-candidates rather than folded in — it's a genuinely distinct non-corporate context, not noise.
- Six off-angle PAA items and six off-angle related searches were dropped as noise (stress/quitting/career-advice questions, geographic/hourly/company-specific pay cuts) — none bear on the per-hire commission angle.

## Weak / uncertain
- The competitive set is thin and mostly generic: of 9 distinct ranking pages, only 3 (trykondo, gogpac, betterleap) actually discuss pay *structure* rather than a single salary number — Coursera/ZipRecruiter/Indeed/Reddit/Quora/YouTube contribute nothing structural. That's a real gap to win, but it also means the "winners" read-list is small (3 pages), so the gap analysis rests on limited direct competitive evidence.
- gogpac is a strong incumbent by depth (14.6k words) and gets closest to this asset's four-way model split with its "Salary vs Commission" framing, though it still stops at two-way, not four. It also drifts into off-topic blog content (AI trainers, interview tips, recruitment pitch) — so its real on-topic depth is less than 14.6k words suggests.
- Heading parser caps around 12-15 entries per page: gogpac (14.6k words, only 12 headings captured) and Betterleap (6.3k words, 4 headings, 3 of them repeated CTAs) likely have more real subtopic structure than what was captured. Treat the "Common H2s" list as a floor, not a ceiling.
- No featured snippet exists for the primary — open opportunity, but also means there's no incumbent snippet copy to benchmark against or beat directly.
- AI Overview is present-but-volatile (personalized, may not show for every user) and does not cite Testlify — a GEO gap, but the AIO's own structure (corporate → agency → executive search → compensation-model table → fee mechanics → top industries) is worth checking against the final draft's structure since it's clearly the model users see first.

## Step 6
- AEO/AI-answer-landscape check returned no usable signal — all 15 direct questions and 12 fan-out queries drifted to job-seeker hiring/visa topics, none touching recruiter commission structure. The corpus simply doesn't cover this topic from that angle, so this method added nothing this run. Standing recommendation: treat Step 6 as low-yield by default for recruiter-compensation and similar B2B-analytical/niche-professional-comp topics where the AI corpus skews toward job-seeker intent — don't burn cycles re-running it without a materially different query set.

## Follow-ups
1. Protect the four-way model comparison table (agency/contingency, retained, RPO, in-house) as the asset's structural spine — no competitor page structures the comparison this way, and it's the single clearest gap versus gogpac's weaker two-way salary/commission split.
2. Source commission percentage benchmarks (e.g., 20% vs. 25% contingency fee) from public industry-fee data rather than repeating a single competitor's claim — this was flagged explicitly as a gap and matches the angle's "public salary and industry-fee data" promise.
3. Build out the retained-search (retainer %, milestone/upfront splits) and RPO recruiter-pay sections in full — both are named in the angle but absent from all three competitor heading sets, making them the cleanest differentiation opportunities.
4. Give role/seniority dollar ranges as structured tables, not narrative — Kondo covers career stage only through anecdotal "stories"; a real table format is a stated gap and matches the angle's "by role and seniority" scope.
5. Include a short featured-snippet-targeted definition (40-60 words) near the top answering the primary keyword directly — no incumbent currently holds the snippet.
6. When drafting, don't over-index on gogpac's depth (14.6k words) as the bar to match — a meaningful share of that length is off-topic drift (recruitment pitch, unrelated blog content); match its on-topic structural coverage, not its raw word count.
