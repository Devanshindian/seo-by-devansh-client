# Research doc — How Much Recruiters Actually Make Per Hire

> **Asset:** How Much Recruiters Actually Make Per Hire: Commission Rates by Recruiter Type in 2026
>
> **Distinct angle:** Breaks down what 'per hire' pay really means across agency/contingency, retained, RPO, and in-house recruiters, with real commission percentages and dollar ranges by role and seniority from public salary and industry-fee data, answering the recruiter's-own-earnings question the keyword actually asks instead of restating employer cost-per-hire.  [spoke keyword: 'how much do recruiters make per hire']

---

## Verdict

- **Play:** the core cluster tops out at 1600 volume with KD 6, a big head keyword at negligible difficulty, so this is a volume play that doesn't need heavy domain authority to contest.
- **Beatable, head:** the SERP is mostly generic salary-lookup pages (Coursera, ZipRecruiter, Indeed) plus thin forum/video slots (Reddit, Quora, YouTube); only three pages (gogpac, Kondo, Betterleap) discuss pay structure at all, and gogpac holds the closest structural attempt with a two-way salary-vs-commission split, nothing near a four-model breakdown.
- **Beatable, long-tail:** the secondary cluster (IT recruiter, HR recruiter, physician recruiter, corporate recruiter, executive recruiter, headhunter pay) sits almost entirely at KD 0, and each maps to a role or recruiter-type this angle already covers by design.
- **The opening:** no ranked page structures per-hire pay across all four engagement models with sourced dollar ranges; retained-search and RPO pay are missing from every real competitor's heading set, and role/seniority detail exists only as anecdote (Kondo's career-stage "stories"), never as a table.

## What the winners cover

**Confirmed format:** Definitional/deep-guide, split into a Q&A-style breakdown of pay by employment type, plus (on one page) narrative case examples; deep — gogpac ~14.6k words, Kondo ~12.0k words, Betterleap ~6.3k words.

**Common H2s (most competitors have):**
- Agency vs. in-house recruiter pay compared side by side
- Salary vs. commission structure explained
- Per-placement / per-hire commission specifics
- In-house recruiter pay broken out separately
- Commission models walked through in detail

**Where the winners drift:**
- gogpac pivots twice into unrelated blog content (AI trainers, interview tips, remote onboarding) and into a "become a recruiter at gpac" recruitment pitch, neither of which answers the pay question.
- Betterleap's headings are dominated by a repeated "meet your recruiting agent" CTA, reading more like a product pitch than a pay breakdown.

**Gaps we can own:**
- Retained-search recruiter pay (retainer %, milestone/upfront splits) — absent from all three heading sets.
- RPO recruiter pay model — not named on any page.
- Role- and seniority-specific dollar ranges in a structured table rather than anecdote (Kondo covers career stage but through narrative "stories," not $ ranges).
- A single table comparing per-hire pay across agency/contingency, retained, RPO, and in-house — no page structures the comparison this way; Kondo only splits agency vs. in-house, gogpac splits salary vs. commission but not by engagement model.
- Sourced commission percentage benchmarks (e.g., 20% vs. 25% contingency fee) tied to public industry-fee data rather than a single site's claim.

## Build spec

- **Word band:** 7,000-9,000 words. Confirmed format is a definitional deep-guide with narrative case examples; real competitors run 6.3k-14.6k words, and the low end (Betterleap) under-covers while the high end (gogpac) pads with off-topic pivots, so aim for full coverage of four models plus role/seniority tables without that bloat.
- **Structure:** primary keyword in the H1 (≤60 chars), in the first 100 words, in 2-3 H2s, and in the conclusion; 4-7 H2s total, at least half question-phrased; every section opens with a 40-60 word liftable answer before expanding.
- **Featured-snippet target:** no snippet currently held, open slot. Lead with a 40-60 word definition splitting recruiter pay into salary vs. commission across the four models, framed to answer "how much do recruiters make" directly in the opening block.
- **Primary sources to cite:** American Staffing Association (industry contingency/retained fee benchmarks), Bureau of Labor Statistics (recruiter/HR specialist wage data), and aggregated comp data (Payscale or Glassdoor) for role- and seniority-level ranges, so commission percentages read as sourced industry figures rather than a single competitor's claim.
- **Close:** end on how recruiters raise their own per-hire and per-placement value, not a recap, then point to Testlify's skills assessments as the lever that shortens time-to-fill and cuts mis-hire risk, the two levers that directly protect a recruiter's commission on every placement.
