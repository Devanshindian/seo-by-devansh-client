## Verdict

- **Play:** Modest-volume, low-difficulty traffic play — primary "cost per hire" clears at 210 vol / KD 7, backed by four near-duplicate variations (170-210 vol, KD 7-18) that widen the same demand without needing separate pages.
- **Beatable — head:** Top 10 is HR-consulting blogs, HRIS/vendor glossaries, and one association page (SHRM) — no forum or thin-content outliers, but also no page with real calculation depth beyond SHRM's flat industry-average figure, which this angle is built to move past.
- **Beatable — long-tail:** "calculate cost per hire" (110 vol, KD 4) and "total cost of job formula" (110 vol, KD 0) are near-zero-difficulty secondary terms — each can anchor its own H2 and rank fast.
- **The opening:** Nobody segments benchmarks by role and hiring volume, nobody names an explicit hard-cost/soft-cost split (everyone stops at internal/external), and no calculator ties back to a broader cost-per-hire pillar — this asset owns all three gaps hiretruffle and juicebox get closest to but don't close.

## Build spec

- **Word band:** 6,000-7,500 words to match the confirmed definitional-plus-how-to guide format (competitors run 6.3k-7.8k words) without matching juicebox's off-topic product-pitch bloat.
- **Structure:** primary "cost per hire" in the H1 (≤60 chars) and first 100 words; woven into 2-3 H2s and the conclusion; 4-7 H2s total, ≥50% question-phrased (e.g. "How do you calculate cost per hire?"); every section opens with a 40-60 word liftable answer before expanding.
- **Featured-snippet target:** a 40-60 word definition of cost per hire (formula + what it measures) — snippet is open, no competitor holds it.
- **Primary sources to cite:** SHRM's Cost-Per-Hire Standard (the benchmark the article explicitly improves on), U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics wage/occupational data (to ground role-based benchmark ranges), and one recruiting-industry benchmarking report (e.g. LinkedIn Talent Solutions or a similar annual hiring-cost survey) for the by-volume cost curve.
- **Close:** forward next step — build your own cost-per-hire number using the line items above — paired with one Testlify CTA tied to the angle (skills-based screening cuts the interview-time and mis-hire soft costs this calculation surfaces), not a recap of the article.
