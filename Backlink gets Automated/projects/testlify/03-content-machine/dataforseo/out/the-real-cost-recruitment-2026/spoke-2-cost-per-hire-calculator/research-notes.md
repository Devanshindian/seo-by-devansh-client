# Research notes — Cost Per Hire (agent commentary)

*Separate from the clean brief. What happened, calls made, what's weak, follow-ups.*

## Judgment calls
- Primary landed on `cost per hire` (vol 210, KD 7) over the higher-KD `costs per hire` (KD 13) and the lower-volume `cost of hire` (KD 7, vol 170) — it's the exact head term for the calculation topic and clears the KD ceiling under commercial intent, so no real contest here.
- `costs per hire`, `cost of hire`, `cost to hire`, `hire cost` all folded in as variations rather than each getting its own section — they're rewords of the same intent, not distinct sub-topics, and forcing separate sections for them would have manufactured structure that doesn't exist in the SERP.
- `calculating cost per hire` was folded into `calculate cost per hire` as a near-duplicate rather than kept separate — same search intent, no reason to split.
- `cost per hire dashboard` (a related search) was flagged off-angle and excluded from keywords, but kept as a demand signal for a CTA/lead-magnet nod — searchers wanting a tracking dashboard aren't the DIY-calculation reader this piece targets, but it's a real adjacent need worth a soft mention.
- shrm.org ranks #3 but was deliberately excluded from the read-list — reading it would mean sourcing from the exact generic industry-average figure this asset exists to move past. That's a judgment call, not an oversight: differentiating from a source means not leaning on it for structure.
- Five in-body-only terms (hard costs, soft costs, cost per hire by industry, cost per hire benchmark, SHRM average cost per hire) carry no keyword because none clear the volume/KD floor alone, but they're central to the angle's line-item framework — they stay in the body without inflating the keyword table.

## Weak / uncertain
- The keyword head is thin. Primary volume is 210, and every variation/secondary sits in the 100-210 range — this is a low-search-volume topic overall, not a head-term goldmine. Expect modest traffic ceiling even if the article wins the SERP gap.
- Incumbents are substantive, not thin content to easily out-rank: hiretruffle (~7.8k words), aihr (~7.5k words), juicebox (~6.3k words) all go deep on formula + benchmarks. The open gap (role/volume-segmented benchmarks, explicit hard/soft split, calculator tied to a pillar) is real, but it's a differentiation-of-structure play, not a case of beating weak content.
- Heading parse capped at ~15 per page, and both hiretruffle and juicebox show signs of truncation (hiretruffle ends mid-stats-heading with no closing/FAQ section; juicebox's last heading reads like a displaced FAQ item). The "gaps we can own" list is built on what the parser saw, not necessarily the complete page — there could be a benchmark-by-role section further down that got cut off the parse window on either competitor.
- AI Overview already gives a full formula + cost breakdown (Internal/External costs, $4,500-$4,800 baseline, $15k-30k+ for specialized/C-suite roles) citing five other domains, none of them Testlify — this is a live GEO gap, but also confirms the "definition + formula" ground is already well-covered by others; the win has to come from the depth/segmentation the angle promises, not from covering new territory.
- Featured snippet is open (no one holds it) — good opportunity, but it means winning it is unproven, not a guaranteed capture.

## Follow-ups
1. Build the role-segmented and hiring-volume-segmented benchmark tables explicitly as their own structural element — this is the single clearest gap versus all three winners, who each give one flat benchmark block.
2. Name the hard-cost/soft-cost split as its own section header, distinct from the internal/external cost framing every competitor already uses — don't let it collapse into a subsection of "internal costs."
3. Protect the five in-body-only terms (hard costs, soft costs, cost per hire by industry, cost per hire benchmark, SHRM average cost per hire) during any later trimming pass — they carry the angle's substance even though no keyword anchors them.
4. Tie the calculator back to the pillar's broader cost breakdown per the angle, rather than letting it stand alone the way hiretruffle's does.
5. Cover the four on-angle PAA questions (especially "How to calculate cost per hire in SHRM?" and "How is CPH calculated?") as FAQ material, and use the SHRM standard as a cited reference point rather than a leaned-on source for the calculation method itself.
6. Consider a light, non-disruptive nod toward "cost per hire dashboard" demand (e.g., a CTA) without pulling the article off its DIY-calculation angle.
