# Testlify Reader Personas

> A persona is the READER we write TO — never the author byline. Think about the persona;
> NEVER name or address them explicitly in the article. (persona.workflow.md, the one rule)

| Persona | Who | Reads | Cares about | Depth & angle | Not this |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **TA / Recruiting Practitioner** | In-house recruiter or talent acquisition specialist, individual contributor to team lead, SMB through enterprise, screening high volumes (often frontline/hourly roles) with a 42-day average fill time | Screening and pipeline how-tos, hiring-process breakdowns (e.g. 'Walmart hiring process' — them, because they run the funnel it describes), assessment-tool comparisons and alternatives pages, candidate-experience and anti-cheating content, ATS integration pieces | Cutting time-to-hire and cost-per-hire; catching resume manipulation and interview cheating; keeping candidate experience strong at volume | Peer-to-peer coach register, second person, hands-on. Assume they know recruiting vocabulary (screening in/out, drop-off, quality-of-hire) but not I/O psychology. Convince with exact operational numbers (55% faster time-to-hire, 42-day fills, $15,000 bad hire) and a runnable step list they can use today | Board-level ROI framing, legal treatises, or explaining what an ATS is — they live in one. Anything without a concrete next step feels like a waste of their short time |
| **Hiring Manager (non-HR-native)** | A functional lead — engineering manager, ops supervisor, retail or trades manager — who owns one role's hire and partners with recruiting; any company size; hiring is not their day job | 'How to assess for [role/skill]' pieces, role-specific interview-question lists with 'look for:' guidance (e.g. '7 interview questions to check if the candidate is tech-savvy' — them, because they conduct that interview), skill-test explainers, role-responsibility catalog pages (welder, HVAC technician) | Whether this specific candidate can actually do the job; not wasting interview hours; avoiding the bad hire they personally have to manage afterward | Explain assessment concepts simply and in their trade's language, with role-specific examples — they know the job cold but not hiring terminology, so define terms like 'validated test' or 'structured interview' in passing. Convince with job-realistic proof: what the test actually asks, what a good answer looks like | HR jargon (adverse impact, requisition, TA funnel metrics) used unexplained; process-scale content about pipelines of thousands; anything that assumes they hire more than a few times a year |
| **HR / Talent-Management Leader** | HR manager to head of people, mid-size to large company, running talent reviews, succession planning, internal mobility, and retention programs beyond the hiring funnel | Strategy and advice content ('How to perform a talent review in 2026 [in 5 simple steps]' — them, because they own that process), skills-gap and workforce trend pieces, myth-busting industry articles, upskilling and leadership-pipeline content | Building the future-leader bench (63% of companies worry about this); closing the skills gap (87% have or expect one); making people processes fair and consistent, not just fast | Question-led, stat-backed, both-sides-honest — the talent-review and personality-hire register. They think in programs and quarters, not requisitions. Convince with named research (Gallup, McKinsey, PwC) and frameworks they can present internally | Pure product punchiness or pricing talk; how-to-screen-faster tactics (that's the recruiter's problem); condescending explainers of basics like 'what is succession planning' without a fresh angle |
| **Executive & Compliance Buyer** | CHRO, CXO, or legally minded enterprise HR (including OFCCP-covered contractors), signing off on the purchase and answerable for the selection process in court or the boardroom | Business-case and ROI content, compliance and defensibility pieces (the diversity-candidate glossary register — them, because they carry the adverse-impact risk), vendor comparisons at decision stage, security/certification and pricing pages | Total cost and measurable ROI of the hiring stack; a selection process defensible under EEOC Uniform Guidelines; enterprise risk — SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001, GDPR, CCPA | Measured, precise, lawyer-passable language — 'reduce bias,' never 'bias-free.' Convince with named certifications, exact commercial terms (credit-based pricing, 30-day refund), documented-scoring and audit-trail specifics, and Walmart-scale proof (500,000 hires a year) | Chirpy coaching tone, absolute fairness claims, unsourced stats, or step-by-step tactics — they delegate execution and read for risk, cost, and proof |

## How to pick the persona for an article
Built once from the brand-voice.md Audience section; rebuild only when that section changes. One article = exactly one persona, tagged at planning time — write TO that reader but never name or address them explicitly, because naming fences the piece to one reader when others will read it too. Route by topic + intent to whoever decides or acts on it: screening/process/tool-comparison how-tos → Recruiter; 'how to assess for [role/skill]' and role interview questions → Hiring Manager; talent reviews, skills gap, workforce strategy → HR/Talent Leader; ROI, compliance, security, pricing, buying decisions → Executive & Compliance Buyer. Only for genuinely shared how-to topics, default to the more junior hands-on reader (the numbers still satisfy the senior one). Candidates and test-takers who land on interview-question pages are out of scope — never write to them, even when they dominate the search traffic; keep the piece useful to the tagged hiring-side persona. After drafting, verify the tag: if any section would trip the chosen persona's not_this line, re-pitch that section or re-tag the article.
