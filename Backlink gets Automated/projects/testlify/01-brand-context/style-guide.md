# Testlify Style Guide

## Grammar & Mechanics

### Capitalization
- **Headlines & subheadings**: Sentence case for article titles, H2s, and H3s ("Comprehensive guide to skill testing: all you need to understand"). Exception: glossary page titles keep Title Case for the term itself ("Blue-Collar Worker", "Appointment Schedule"). Some older numbered H3s are fully lowercase ("1. financial management"); that is an artifact, not a style. Sentence case them.
- **After a colon**: lowercase what follows a colon unless it is a complete sentence or a proper noun. "Skill testing: all you need to understand" stays lowercase; "One rule matters: Every stat gets a source" takes a capital.
- **Product names**: Testlify is always one word with a capital T only, never all-caps or hyphenated; possessive is fine ("Testlify's pre-employment assessments"). Feature and category phrases stay lowercase ("skills assessment platform", "cognitive ability tests", "hiring plan"). Third-party brands follow the owner's casing (Workday, McDonald's, LinkedIn, O*NET).
- **Industry terms** (house spelling and casing):
  - pre-employment assessment (hyphenated, lowercase)
  - skills-based hiring, skills assessment, skills test (always plural "skills"; never "skill test")
  - talent acquisition (lowercase; "TA" after first use)
  - power skills (Testlify's branded term for soft skills; lowercase)
  - work-life balance (hyphenated)
  - workforce management (WFM), workforce planning, headcount (one word)
  - non-exempt (hyphenated; not "nonexempt")
  - time-to-hire, time-to-productivity (hyphenated)
  - onboarding, offboarding, frontline, workweek (one word)
  - e-commerce (hyphenated)
  - blue-collar, white-collar, pink-collar: hyphenated as a modifier, always ("blue-collar roles"); open only as a standalone noun ("blue collar")
  - full-time, part-time, at-will, pre-tax, high-volume: hyphenated as modifiers
  - culture fit: pick one form and hold it per article; do not alternate "cultural fit" and "cultural fitness"
  - US spelling throughout: organization, labor, customize, judgment, recognize. UK forms (labour, misdemeanour, organising) appear in some older glossary entries; treat them as errors to fix, except in proper names ("Labour Force Participation Rate" as a UK statistical term).

### Numbers
- Spell out one–nine; use numerals for 10 and above.
- Always numerals for: percentages (5%), money ($500), measurements (5 GB), and stats/lists (for scannability). The blogs lean numeral-heavy even below 10 ("3-5 days", "6 core components"); in stats, ranges, and list items that is correct — in running prose, spell one–nine.
- Large numbers: use commas (1,000+); spell out "million"/"billion" in prose. K/M shorthand is fine in stats and data callouts (500K+, $500K–$2M) — pick one style per article.
- Ranges: hyphenate with no spaces (2-3 weeks, 15-20%, $80,000-$120,000). Never open a sentence with a numeral range; recast ("Between 15% and 20% of candidates…" or restructure so the range sits mid-sentence).
- In headings, use the % symbol with a numeral (never "percent"): "Why 45% of new hires fail".
- Currency: $ for US figures; ₹ for India-market figures ($4,700; ₹1,500). Don't write "Rs".
- Multipliers as digit+x (1.5x, 200-300x).

### Punctuation
- **Oxford comma**: Yes — apply consistently.
- **Em dashes**: Use the closed em dash (no surrounding spaces) sparingly for asides: "the right people—with the right skills—are available". Hyphens handle compound modifiers and numeric ranges. Two artifacts from older posts are banned outright: the spaced en dash (" – ") standing in for a dash, and the spaced colon (" : ") where a dash or colon belongs ("An EIN : also called a FEIN"). Fix both on sight.
- **Quotation marks**: Curly (smart) quotes — double for quoted phrases and scare quotes, single for terms ('no-poach'). No straight quotes in published copy.
- **Ellipses**: three dots, no surrounding spaces, used sparingly (mainly for omitted text in quotes). The corpus barely uses them, and the few that appear are accidental double-periods, not stylistic choices — fix those as typos. End open lists with "etc." or a completed phrase, not a trailing "…".

### Abbreviations & Acronyms
- Spell out on first use, then use the acronym: "Applicant Tracking System (ATS)".
- **Common industry acronyms** (define on first use): ATS, HRIS, HCM, WFM, LMS, SJT, KSA, PTO, FTE, DEI, SHRM, EEOC, FLSA, FMLA, ADA, ADEA, OSHA, NLRB, FCRA, GDPR, ACA, COBRA, FICA, HRA, HSA, FSA, HDHP, ERP, CRM, ESS, MSS, O*NET (that casing, not "O*Net"), SOC, BLS, DOL, IRS, TIN, SSN, EIN. C-suite titles (CEO, CHRO, CTO) need no expansion.
- Latin abbreviations: use "e.g.", "i.e.", "etc." sparingly.

## Word Choice & Usage

### Preferred Terms — "Say This → Not That"
- organizations → not companies/firms as the default collective
- candidates → not applicants (except in volume-screening contexts)
- employees → not staff; "workers" only in safety and labor-law contexts (and "associates" when quoting Walmart's own usage)
- power skills → not soft skills (Testlify's branded term)
- skills-based hiring → not skill-based hiring
- skills test / skills assessment → not skill test
- pre-employment assessment → not pre-hire test
- hiring managers, recruiters, TA teams → the reader is the hirer; address them as "you"
- workforce → as the collective noun; tenure → for length of service
- structured → the workhorse modifier (structured interviews, structured hiring plan)
- data-driven, objective → for assessment claims (but back them with a stat, not just the adjective)
- Reserve "we" for Testlify speaking about Testlify ("we built the Cognitive Ability Test to…"); never the authorial "In this article, we will explore". Add other terms marketing prefers or avoids — positioning language, launch vocabulary — *confirm with marketing*.

### Words to Avoid
- Cut filler: "very", "really", "actually" (usually unnecessary).
- No "click here" / "read more" — use descriptive link text.
- No AI-clichés / hype: streamline, seamless(ly), leverage, robust, empower, elevate, enhance, crucial, essential, comprehensive, holistic, delve, realm, unravel, foster, harness, cornerstone/backbone, dynamic, fast-paced, ever-evolving, future-proof, must-have, powerful, Kickstart, endeavors.
- Banned openers and tics: "In today's rapidly evolving/fast-paced landscape", "It's important to note that", "Overall," as a paragraph opener, stacked "Additionally,/Furthermore," openers, the "Remember," paragraph-opener tic, "Let's explore", "Let's be honest", "In simple words / In short / In a word", "But don't worry!", "there you have it".
- Banned constructions and images: "it's not just X; it's Y", "win-win for everyone", "hiring game", "beacon of hope", "needle in a haystack", "on top of their game", "renewed energy and creativity".
- Boilerplate to retire: "attract and retain top talent" and "drives improvement/results" — both pasted near-verbatim across multiple posts; "informed decisions", "significantly" as an all-purpose intensifier, "valuable" as an all-purpose adjective.
- Never paste a dangling citation fragment mid-sentence ("SHRM's workforce planning guidance" as a sentence stub). Complete the sentence or cut it.

### Inclusive Language
- Gender-neutral where possible; people-first language; avoid idioms that don't translate.

## Formatting Standards
### Text Formatting
- Bold for genuine emphasis (sparingly); italics for terms/titles; no underline (reads as a link); no ALL CAPS.
### Lists
- Bulleted for non-sequential items; numbered for steps/rankings; nested lists indented one level; items parallel; one idea each.
### Links
- Descriptive anchor text (never "click here"); link the phrase that names the destination.
### Code & Technical Elements
- `inline code` for short snippets / UI labels; fenced blocks for multi-line code.
### Callout Boxes / Asides
- Use sparingly for tips, warnings, or key takeaways.

## Content Structure
### Article Introduction
- Hook → the reader's problem → the promise of the article (≈150–250 words).
- The intro must satisfy the answer-first + TL;DR spec in `seo-aeo-geo-guidelines.md` — that document owns the structural rules; this guide does not restate them.
### Section Length
- One idea per section; ~200-300 words; clear H2/H3 hierarchy; scannable.
- Serial headings stay grammatically parallel: if one H2 in a series is a question, they all are; if one starts with a gerund ("Building your scorecard"), its siblings match.
### Conclusion
- Forward-looking close with one clear CTA (≈150-200 words). No recap of what the article said — the no-recap rule in `seo-aeo-geo-guidelines.md` governs, and it supersedes any "summarize the value" habit.

## SEO-Specific Style
### Meta Titles
- ≤ 60 characters, primary keyword near the front.
### Meta Descriptions
- ~150-160 characters, includes the keyword, reads as a benefit.
### URL Slugs
- Short, lowercase, hyphenated, keyword-focused; no stop-words / dates.
### Alt Text
- Describe the image; include the keyword only when natural.

## Dates & Time
- Format: "Month DD, YYYY" (e.g. July 4, 2026). Consistent, unambiguous formats; avoid "5/4". No ordinals ("January 31", not "January 31st").
- Localization: keep US spelling and the Month DD, YYYY date format everywhere, including India- and UK-audience posts. The only localized element is currency (₹ for India figures). If marketing later wants localized editions, that becomes a separate rule set — don't improvise per post.

## Statistics & Data
### Citing Sources
- Cite the source inline and link it; name the source; don't invent or misround.
- Attribute every stat to a named source, using a full sentence pattern: "According to SHRM, …" or "A McKinsey study found…". Never leave a source name hanging as a fragment.
### Presenting Numbers
- Numerals for scannability; keep units consistent.

## Images & Media
### Image Captions
- Where they add context; brief and specific.
### Screenshots
- Current, legible, annotated where helpful.
### Charts & Graphs
- Labelled axes/units and a clear title; source cited.

## Brand-Specific Guidelines
### Testlify Product References
- Testlify: one word, capital T, never all-caps. Generic features and categories stay lowercase ("skills assessment platform", "pre-employment assessments", "hiring plan").
- Named tests and frameworks take Title Case: "Cognitive Ability Test", "Candidate Data Profile (CDP)".
- Link to the relevant product or test page whenever you name a specific test.
- For future features that don't yet have a settled public name — *confirm with marketing*.
### Competitor References
- Name competitors in their own native casing: TestGorilla, Adaface, Mercer Mettl, iMocha, HireVue, HackerRank. Comparisons stay fair, factual, and free of disparagement.
- Never cite or link a direct assessment competitor as a source: Adaface, Criteria, eSkill, iMocha, Mettl, TestDome, TestGorilla, Vervoe, WeCP (rule sourced from `seo-aeo-geo-guidelines.md`). Their stats and blog posts do not appear in our content, even attributed.
- Any additional do/don't rules (naming in titles, comparison-page tone) — *confirm with marketing*.

## Accessibility
### Screen Reader Friendly
- Real heading hierarchy, alt text on images, descriptive link text.
### Plain Language
- Short sentences; define jargon on first use.

## Voice & Tone Reminders
### Core Voice Characteristics
- Confident, second-person, proof-backed, plain-spoken. The reader is the hirer; talk to them directly, back claims with named stats, and skip the throat-clearing. The register to imitate: "Start with the work, not the wish list." Short declarative openers like that, then the evidence. (The full voice lives in `brand-voice.md`.)
### Tone Variations
- How tone shifts by content type — see `brand-voice.md` (Tone Guidelines).

## Editing Checklist
Before publishing: spelling/grammar checked · punctuation consistent (Oxford comma, dashes, quotes) · numbers &
dates consistent · preferred terminology used · formatting consistent · headings hierarchical · strong intro +
CTA · keyword integrated naturally · meta + slug + alt text done · internal/external links working · sources cited
· genuinely useful · ready to publish.

## Updates & Maintenance
- Keep the guide current: add new terms/acronyms as they appear, revisit when products or positioning change, and keep it aligned with `brand-voice.md`.
- Precedence when documents conflict: `brand-voice.md` owns voice and tone; `seo-aeo-geo-guidelines.md` owns article structure and SEO/AEO rules (intro, conclusion, competitor-citation ban); this style guide owns mechanics (spelling, casing, punctuation, numbers, terminology). Resolve a conflict by editing the lower-precedence document to match, in the same change — never leave two documents disagreeing.
