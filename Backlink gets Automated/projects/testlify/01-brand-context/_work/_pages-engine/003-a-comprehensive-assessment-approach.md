## Assessment approach
Testlify employs a comprehensive assessment approach for evaluating software engineers, focusing on code-based test items. While extensive research supports the effectiveness of work-sample approaches for selection, there is a limited body of research specifically tailored to software engineers. This scarcity can be attributed to several factors:
- Newness to psychometric testing: Computer coding content and web-based assessments are relatively new in the field of psychometric testing, which may explain the limited research in this area.
- Limited value to academia: Traditional tests assessing declarative (know it) and procedural (do it) knowledge offer little value to academia, leading to a lack of emphasis on specific research in this domain.
- Proprietary examples: Many applied examples of code-based assessments are proprietary and intentionally concealed from the public, further restricting access to research and publication.
The second factor likely plays a significant role in the limited research on code-based assessments for software engineers. However, it is widely recognized that computer-based assessments effectively evaluate coding skills. This raises the question of confirmation bias, as some may question the necessity of testing for coding skills that are seemingly self-evident.
Scoring methodology: Testlify employs a scoring system that reflects criteria characteristic of programmer performance in a professional setting. The technical component of the assessment incorporates aspects of efficiency and efficacy, considering the following factors:
- Time to solve: Measures how quickly candidates can solve coding challenges.
- Memory usage: Assesses how efficiently candidates compose their code.
- Accuracy of solution: Evaluates the effectiveness of the solution in terms of accuracy.
The assessment acknowledges that accuracy is limited to a binary “right/wrong” distinction.
## Standardization
Standardization is a fundamental aspect of Testlify’s design. With a content-referenced approach to validity, ensuring standardization is of utmost importance. While addressing cheating in remote administration is essential, Testlify goes beyond typical un-proctored assessments to achieve comprehensive standardization.
Testlify is guided by four principles that underpin its approach to standardized assessment:
- Minimizing confirmation bias: Testlify recognizes the addictive nature of resumes and CVs for hiring parties. To combat this bias, Testlify employs testing approach, using simulations as a reliable verification of claimed competence in specialized skills. Additionally, candidate information is reserved until after the completion of the entire assessment, preventing undue influence on the evaluation process.
- Mitigating cheating: Testlify employs rigorous measures to minimize cheating. Each candidate test session is recorded from the start of the assessment until its submission, reducing the likelihood of remote logging, information referencing beyond the test’s terms, multiple respondents, and other means that could compromise the accuracy of scores.
- Maximizing completion rates: Ensuring fair use of the assessment requires attention to completion rates. Systematic patterns of assessment dropouts can introduce bias and hinder standardization. Testlify addresses this by providing equal access and treatment to candidates. Since the assessment can be administered at any time and place, with the widespread availability of required technology among programmers, equal access is virtually guaranteed. Furthermore, presenting the assessment with candidate convenience in mind increases the likelihood of higher completion rates.
- Candidate feedback: Although not a formal demonstration of fair treatment, feedback from candidates provides valuable insights. The assessment includes interview questions that allow candidates to freely comment on their experience. This feedback can uncover factors that may pose legitimate challenges. As the candidate’s assessment session is recorded from beginning to end, false claims can be identified, and reports of mistreatment or procedural inequity have not been substantiated.
The overall feedback from candidates indicates a high level of satisfaction with the Testlify assessment. They appreciate its alignment with the nature of the job they are applying for (working with computer code) and value the convenience it offers in terms of completion.
## Summary
Accurately assessing candidates for various roles presents a set of challenges that can significantly impact decision-making in high-stakes scenarios. These challenges include:
- Uninformed decisions: Given the importance of hiring for any position, making uninformed decisions can have severe consequences for organizations. The lack of accurate assessment methods increases the risk of selecting candidates who may not meet the required criteria or perform well in the role.
- Knowledge gap in the field: The rapidly evolving nature of various fields creates a knowledge gap between different organizational levels. Without a deep understanding of the specific domain, leaders may rely on superficial evaluations based solely on labels or credentials, potentially overlooking crucial aspects of candidates’ capabilities.
- Overlooking critical competencies: Focusing solely on specific skill sets or technical knowledge gaps between the hiring party and candidates can lead to overlooking other essential competencies. Successful professionals require a range of skills beyond technical expertise, including communication, problem-solving, and collaboration. Neglecting these competencies can result in suboptimal hiring decisions.
- Confirmation bias: Conditions are ripe for confirmation bias in candidate assessments. Past experiences, educational backgrounds, or certifications can influence evaluators’ perceptions, leading to false assumptions about a candidate’s competence. Confirmation bias can prevent objective evaluation and undermine the selection process.
By addressing these challenges and implementing comprehensive assessment methods, organizations can improve their decision-making processes and increase the likelihood of selecting the most suitable candidates for a wide range of roles.
