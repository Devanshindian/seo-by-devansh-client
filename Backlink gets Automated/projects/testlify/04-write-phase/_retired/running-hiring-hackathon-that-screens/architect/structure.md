# STRUCTURE — Running a Hiring Hackathon That Screens for Real Skill, With a Judging Rubric and an AI-Use Policy

**Format:** how-to-guide · **Sections:** 10 · **Authored H3s:** 10 · **Boxes used:** 50


**The article's spine:** A hiring hackathon is a work sample test, so the thing that decides whether it produces a usable hiring signal is not the event but the scoring: a rubric with fixed criteria, weights and anchored bands written before the event, a panel calibrated against sample submissions and checked for agreement, and a published AI-use policy that says what candidates may generate, what they must disclose and how that disclosure is scored. Written for the in-house recruiter or TA lead running this at volume, who should finish able to build the rubric, run the calibration session and publish the AI policy for their next event.

**What it left out, and why:** Left out the ranking pages' event-logistics ground (in-person expos vs online/hybrid judging formats, demo-day scheduling, prize framing) because it belongs to public prize hackathons, not a hiring screen. Left out 'what makes a project stand out' and most participant-facing advice because it is written for the candidate, not the recruiter running the funnel. Google's four-criterion list (Innovation, Technology, Impact, Presentation) is not dropped but compressed into one rubric table in section 2, so the article can spend its length on weights, anchors, calibration and the AI-use policy, which no ranking page delivers. 'Common score reducers' survives inside the anchored score bands rather than as its own section.
