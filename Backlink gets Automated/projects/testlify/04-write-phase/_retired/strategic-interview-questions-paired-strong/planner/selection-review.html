The world — what this article is and is not about

The tagger judged every sub-topic against these two lines before anything else. Where it refused one for belonging to a different subject, it says so in the reason below, so read the boundary first.

IS about
Interview questions a hiring manager or recruiter asks candidates for a given role, each paired with a real strong-answer example and a real weak-answer example plus a short line on how to score the response — a rubric interviewers use DURING the interview to judge what a candidate actually said, replacing vague 'look for good communication skills' criteria with something to benchmark against.
is NOT about
Not job-seeker interview prep (articles coaching a candidate on how to answer questions to get hired) — this is written for the interviewer's side of the table, not the candidate's. Not 'strategic interview' in the consulting/case-interview sense (McKinsey/BCG-style case questions testing business strategy or market-sizing skills) — 'strategic' here means questions that probe a candidate's ability to think strategically about their job function, not a case-study interview format. Not corporate strategy or strategic planning content. Not 'interview' in the qualitative-research-methodology sense (structured/unstructured interviews as a data-collection technique) or the journalism sense (an interview as a published Q&A piece). Not clinical or psychological intake interviews. Not general behavioral-question listicles that give the question alone with no paired answer or scoring guidance — the answer-pair-plus-score is the defining feature, not just 'good interview questions.' Not interview scheduling, coordination, or ATS workflow tools.
