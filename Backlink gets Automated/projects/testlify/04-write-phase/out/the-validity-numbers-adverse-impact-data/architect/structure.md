# STRUCTURE — The Validity Numbers and Adverse-Impact Data Behind Psychometric Hiring Tests

**Format:** data-benchmark-report · **Sections:** 8 · **Authored H3s:** 1 · **Boxes used:** 39


**The article's spine:** Every assessment type carries two numbers, a predictive-validity coefficient and a group-difference figure, and this article puts the pair side by side for cognitive ability, personality and situational judgment tests so a CHRO or compliance-accountable HR leader can see which tests actually forecast performance and what each one costs in pass-rate disparity. It then shows how that disparity is measured under the four-fifths rule, what the employer has to prove once it is flagged, where federal enforcement now stands after Executive Order 14281, and which design choices shrink the gap without giving up validity. At the end the reader can defend a specific test battery with named evidence rather than a vendor's fairness claim.

**What it left out, and why:** Google's own answer for this query is the generic disparate-impact doctrine (neutral rules, discriminatory effect, no intent needed, plaintiff's burden, employer's defense, less discriminatory alternative, Griggs/Title VII, recent federal shifts), and the ranking-page list came back empty. I gave that ground two real sections early enough to hold the reader: section 6 covers the measurement threshold, the burden shift, Griggs and the business-necessity defense, and section 7 covers the recent enforcement shift. I dropped only the housing/Fair Housing Act branch of Google's answer, because it sits outside employment law and outside this article's subject. The format's optional provenance block is folded into section 2, which names which publication and year each coefficient comes from and why the 1998 and 2022 figures cannot be compared directly.
