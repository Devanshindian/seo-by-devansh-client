The H1 and the intro

H1
Adverse Impact vs Validity: The Numbers by Test Type
intro
The assessment method that predicts job performance best is also the one that rejects Black candidates at the highest rate. That pattern is what a disparate impact claim is built on: one group passing a screen at a much lower rate than another. Dropping the test and interviewing instead looks like the safe move, and it is not. Unstructured interviews forecast performance less than half as well, and researchers find score gaps just as wide [c134] [c138]. Every assessment type carries two numbers: how well it forecasts performance, and how wide its score gap runs. Put those pairs side by side and you can see which tests you can actually defend.
quick answer
Validity and adverse impact move together. The methods that forecast performance most accurately tend to produce the widest pass-rate gaps between racial groups, and cognitive ability tests sit at that extreme. Situational judgment tests narrow the gap but predict less, and they add little on top of a cognitive test you already run. Personality measures show almost no racial gap, though they carry disability-discrimination risk instead. The gap becomes a legal problem when a group's pass rate falls below 80% of the highest group's. From there you must show the test is job-related, so the answer is a mixed battery, a cutoff set in advance, and a validation file.

The FAQ — 5 question(s)

What is the difference between disparate impact and disparate treatment? the editor found this one37 words
Disparate treatment is intentional: you treat one group differently on purpose. Disparate impact needs no intent. A neutral rule, applied to everyone, screens out one group at a much higher rate and cannot be justified as job-related.
What is an example of disparate impact? from real searches39 words
A neutral screen that rejects one group far more often. In Griggs v. Duke Power Co., a general intelligence test and a diploma requirement screened out Black applicants, with no evidence the abilities were needed for the job [c115].
Can we rely on a vendor's validation study instead of running our own? the editor found this one37 words
Sometimes. Validity evidence for cognitive tests generalizes across jobs, so a fresh local study is not always required [c7]. You still need a documented job analysis showing the vendor's roles match yours, plus your own pass-rate data.
Does an NYC Local Law 144 bias audit make a test legally defensible? the editor found this one37 words
No. The audit is a transparency requirement: an independent check of pass rates, published, with candidate notice [c9029]. It does not prove the test is job-related. That is what Title VII asks for once impact is shown.
How often should we check pass rates by group? the editor found this one32 words
Quarterly at minimum, comparing pass rates across groups [c153]. Rerun the check whenever you change a cutoff, add a test, or open a high-volume role, since small samples swing the ratio easily.
