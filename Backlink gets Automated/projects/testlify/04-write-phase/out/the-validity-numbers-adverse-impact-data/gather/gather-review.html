The world — what this article is and is not about

Google returns questions and searches for the WORDS in a query, not for the audience behind them, so some of what comes back belongs to a different field that happens to share our vocabulary. The two lines below are the boundary the vetting used. Read them before judging the keeps and drops.

IS about
The statistical evidence behind pre-employment psychometric/cognitive-ability and personality assessments: validity coefficients (how well a test score predicts job performance), criterion-related validity studies, and adverse-impact data (the disparate pass/hire rates a test produces across protected groups, e.g. the four-fifths rule, EEOC guidelines, disparate-impact litigation). It covers how employers and I-O psychologists read these numbers to judge whether a hiring test is both predictive and legally defensible.
is NOT about
This is NOT about clinical or medical psychometrics (validity of a diagnostic instrument like a depression or IQ clinical scale, test-retest reliability in a clinical/research psychology sense). It is NOT about general statistical 'validity' in research methodology, survey design, or scientific studies unrelated to hiring. It is NOT about 'adverse impact' or 'adverse events' as used in medicine/pharmacovigilance (drug side effects). It is NOT about credit-scoring or lending 'adverse action' notices, which use similar disparate-impact language under different law (ECOA/FCRA) rather than employment law (Title VII/EEOC). It is NOT about broader DEI or workplace-culture diversity metrics unconnected to a specific test's pass-rate data. It is NOT about personality/psychometric tests used for non-hiring purposes such as team-building, coaching, or consumer personality quizzes (e.g. Myers-Briggs marketed to general audiences). It is NOT about academic testing validity (SAT/GRE admissions test validity), even though the same statistical vocabulary (validity coefficients, adverse impact) is used in that literature.
