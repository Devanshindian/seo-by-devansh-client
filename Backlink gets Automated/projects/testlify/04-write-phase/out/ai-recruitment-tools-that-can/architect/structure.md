# STRUCTURE — AI Recruitment Tools That Can Survive a Bias Audit

**Format:** comparison-rankings · **Sections:** 24 · **Authored H3s:** 27 · **Boxes used:** 91

**Options:** HireVue · Pymetrics · Eightfold AI · Workday · Testlify
**Yardsticks:** published bias-audit evidence · auditor independence and transparency · adverse-impact and intersectional results · what the tool actually scores · candidate notice and disclosure support · legal exposure and vendor contract terms

**The article's spine:** For a CHRO or compliance owner who personally carries the adverse-impact risk, this article argues that the only defensible way to judge an AI recruiting tool is against published audit evidence: an independent auditor, a stated methodology, impact ratios reported by sex, race and their intersections, and a candidate notice you can prove you sent. Measured that way, the five tools in this category separate sharply, and most of them fail on transparency rather than on fairness. By the end the reader can name which of the five has evidence a regulator or a plaintiff's counsel would accept, run the four-fifths math on their own funnel, and tell a real audit summary from a responsible-AI marketing page.

**What it left out, and why:** None of the three expected topics is left out: the numbered one-section-per-tool ranking runs as sections 7 to 11, the tools-at-a-glance table sits at section 6 immediately before the deep dives, and the how-to-choose criteria breakdown runs as section 23 (rebuilt around audit evidence rather than setup speed and pricing, because those are not what this reader signs off on). What is dropped from Google's own answer is its sourcing bucket (Juicebox, hireEz, Fetcher) and its scheduling bucket, which sit outside the screen/match/score/converse category this article audits, plus the ATS-integration and pricing criteria, which no bias audit measures.
