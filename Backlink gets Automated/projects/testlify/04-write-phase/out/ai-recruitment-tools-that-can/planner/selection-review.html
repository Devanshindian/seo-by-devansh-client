The world — what this article is and is not about

The tagger judged every sub-topic against these two lines before anything else. Where it refused one for belonging to a different subject, it says so in the reason below, so read the boundary first.

IS about
Building or buying AI-driven recruitment tools (resume screening, candidate matching, video-interview scoring, chatbot screening) in a way that holds up under a bias/fairness audit — meaning the tool's outputs can be tested for adverse impact across protected groups, its scoring logic can be explained, and it meets audit laws like NYC Local Law 144 or the EEOC's four-fifths rule. The reader is an HR/TA leader or compliance owner deciding which AI hiring tools are defensible, what an audit actually checks, and how to prepare for or pass one.
is NOT about
This is not about AI-based skills assessments or structured pre-employment testing (Testlify's own category) except as a bias-resistant alternative to worry about — the article's job is to cover the audit and compliance landscape for AI recruiting tools broadly, not to pitch assessments. It is NOT about clinical or medical bias audits, NOT about statistical 'bias' in the machine-learning training sense divorced from hiring law, NOT about general DEI or diversity-hiring initiatives unrelated to tool auditing, NOT about financial or algorithmic 'audit' in the accounting/security sense, and NOT about non-hiring HR AI uses (performance review AI, payroll AI, workforce planning AI). It also excludes general AI ethics/regulation discourse (e.g., the EU AI Act in full) except where it specifically governs recruitment tool audits.
