# STRUCTURE — The Resume Statistics Everyone Cites That Are Already Out of Date

**Format:** data-benchmark-report · **Sections:** 14 · **Authored H3s:** 6 · **Boxes used:** 42


**The article's spine:** Almost every resume statistic in circulation is quoted without the year, sample or definition that produced it, so figures measured five years and four methods apart get stacked as if they described one timeless reality. This article dates each headline figure to the survey behind it, separates the ones measuring different things, names the ones with no traceable source at all, and adds the AI-fabrication figures that competing roundups omit. An HR or talent leader should finish able to cite any of these numbers with its year, publisher and method attached, and to drop the ones that cannot survive that test.

**What it left out, and why:** Left out only "Key takeaways / summary block" — that is the TL;DR, which a later step adds, so building it here would duplicate it. The other three expected topics each get a section of their own: recruiter screening time opens the article (S1, with S2 and S3 extending it), rejection rates and reasons get S11, and application-volume gets S12. Google's own SERP answer is ignored entirely: it defines CV as the statistical coefficient of variation, a homonym with no bearing on the hiring document.
