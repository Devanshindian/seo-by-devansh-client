How Long Recruiters Spend on a Resume, and on Which Pass251 → 251 words · avg 14.8 → 13.2

Before
Screening time is not one number. It is a function of how long the document is and which pass you happen to be timing.

In 2023 Jan Tegze put three CVs of different lengths in front of 114 recruiters and timed every review. The shortest averaged 17 seconds. The longest averaged 46 [c5]. Every one of those figures sits well above the six-second benchmark, and the spread between them is the finding, not noise around it. A longer CV genuinely does buy more attention.

It just does not buy it where the candidate hopes. The same study mapped where reviewers looked and found attention concentrated on the first page, with later pages only skimmed [c107]. Page two earns seconds. It does not earn decisions.

The other half of this is what ends a read early. Dense blocks of text and inconsistent formatting accounted for 43% of rejected resumes [c208]. That is a rejection reason with nothing to do with whether the candidate can do the job, and it is the one item on this list you can act on without changing your process at all.

So stop treating screening time as a fixed budget you are up against, and start treating it as something your own setup controls. If your reviewers are working from a PDF buried in an email thread, they are sitting at the low end of that range. If they see the job requirements beside the document, they read for longer and they read further down the page.

So why do applications vanish? Supply. Applications arrived roughly four times faster than roles did. A recruiter works down a ranked list, fills the role, and closes it, and everything below that line goes unread by anybody.

The distinction changes what you actually do. Believe an algorithm is rejecting people and you buy resume-formatting advice for candidates. Understand that the queue is simply longer than the working day and you fix the queue instead: tighter requirements at the top of the funnel, or a short structured task that sorts people by something other than how early they applied.
