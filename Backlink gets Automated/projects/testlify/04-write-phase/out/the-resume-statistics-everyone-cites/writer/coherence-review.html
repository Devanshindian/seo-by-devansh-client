Every earlier step looked at one section at a time. This one holds the whole article at once, so it is the only step that can catch a fault living between sections: a rule stated in section seven and broken in section five, or the same thing scored out of five here and out of four there. It works in two passes. First it lists every rule, scale and number in the article, without judging any of them. Then a second pass gets that list and fixes four things: the article breaking its own rule, advice its own warning covers, one thing scored on two different scales, and two numbers that cannot both be true. It is the only step allowed to rewrite sourced prose, which makes it the riskiest one here.

Employers Reject AI Resumes They Can't Reliably Spot 55.8% of the block rewritten
  • ### 74% say they can spot AI writing; in 2023, 82% could not Employers cannot reliably identify the resumes they say they are rejecting. A 2023 ResumeBuilder survey of 1,000 hiring managers found 82% could not spot a ChatGPT-written cover letter [c36]. Two years later, 74% of hiring managers say they can spot AI-generated content [c37]. Treat that as a straight two-year improvement and you are guessing. The 2023 study tested people against real writing samples and scored whether they were right. The 2025 figure records what hiring managers believe about themselves, which is a different measurement with no scoring attached. Confidence went up; nobody has published evidence that accuracy did. The tools are worse. AI-detection software applied to resumes carries false positive rates of 30-50%, meaning it flags a large share of human-written resumes as machine-made [c155]. A candidate who generates a resume with AI and then edits the word choices, sentence structure, and personal details usually passes the same check [c156]. So the software fails in both directions at once: it accuses honest candidates and clears the ones who took thirty seconds to cover their tracks. The stated giveaway does not hold up either. Half of hiring managers name unnatural phrasing as their main tell [c154]. Phrasing is the one thing an editing pass fixes first, and polished language now arrives regardless of how well a candidate actually communicates. The signal recruiters used to read, awkward wording, has been erased by the same tools they are trying to catch. Detection, then, is not where this gets settled. And whatever a recruiter can or cannot spot, something else reads the resume first. → (cut)
