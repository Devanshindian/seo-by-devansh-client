# Voices from the field, Video Interview Software That Actually Stops Deepfake and Proxy Candidates

> What practitioners say about identifying fake candidates and protecting hiring from deepfake and proxy fraud. This file is research background, never a source or fact claim.

**Coverage.** 7 discussion(s) read and 78 comments, from 1 on linkedin, 6 on reddit. 119 results were looked at across 2 search round(s).

## Deepfakes and proxies are actively happening in hiring

**What they say.** Recruiters report interviewing and hiring people who do not show up to work, discovering during the call that they are AI-generated or a different person than the resume. Multiple practitioners identify North Korean state actors as the source of coordinated proxy-candidate campaigns. One recruiter was duped mid-interview; another hired two fraudulent candidates in succession.

**Strength.** Strong. Appears across 4 separate discussions with consistent details and multiple practitioners confirming they have encountered this firsthand.

**Serves.** Angle and Section 1: Deepfakes and Proxy Candidates Are a Documented Hiring Threat.

**What this changes.** Lead the article with concrete practitioner examples of proxy and deepfake hiring fraud, and make clear that state-sponsored actors (not just individual scammers) are the threat.

## Video verification catches some but not all fraudulent candidates

**What they say.** Recruiters report using video calls as their primary defense, and describe specific checks: asking candidates to wave their hand in front of their face, requesting they hold up a passport or ID next to their face for matching, and asking cultural or region-specific questions to verify real identity. Multiple recruiters report these tactics work on some fakes but not all, and that experienced fraudsters defeat them.

**Strength.** Moderate to strong. Appears in 2–3 discussions, with multiple recruiters comparing their evolving verification tactics.

**Serves.** Section 2: How Vendors Hide What 'Identity Verification' Actually Means.

**What this changes.** Explain that basic video verification (no login, no liveness check, no face matching) is insufficient on its own and that vendor claims of "identity verification" must be unpacked into specific controls (liveness, ID document matching, facial comparison).

## In-person or onsite interview requirement effectively filters out fraudulent candidates

**What they say.** Multiple recruiters report that telling a candidate the job requires on-site or in-person work, or that they will conduct a final interview in person with travel arranged, causes fraudulent candidates to drop out of the process and stop communicating. One recruiter states that no legitimate candidate has objected to the offer of in-person travel and accommodation.

**Strength.** Strong. Appears in multiple discussions from independent recruiters reporting the same outcome.

**Serves.** Section 9: Interview Controls by Role Risk and Cost.

**What this changes.** Include on-site interview requirement as a practical and effective control in the decision framework for roles where the cost of in-person verification is justified by the risk of proxy fraud.

## Practitioners recognize video-only screening is becoming standard but remains insufficient

**What they say.** Recruiters describe phone screening as "dead" and video as the new baseline, but simultaneously acknowledge it is not enough to stop organized fraud. Some are layering video with additional checks (ID verification, cultural questions, LinkedIn profile verification). Others report that speed and volume pressure make it tempting to skip verification steps.

**Strength.** Moderate. Appears in 3 discussions showing the shift to video as expected practice, but recognition that it is incomplete.

**Serves.** Section 2 and Section 9: both the limits of what vendors claim as identity verification, and the controls needed for different scenarios.

**What this changes.** Frame vendors' video screening capabilities as table stakes, not as identity verification; explain that role risk determines which additional controls must layer on top.

## What did not show up

- Vendor comparison of specific identity-verification controls (document verification, liveness detection, face matching) from their security documentation, Reddit practitioners do not audit vendor docs.
- Consent, notice, or GDPR/CCPA compliance requirements for recording and retaining interview video, brief mention of general GDPR concern, no practitioner experience.
- Details on deepfake detection technology or how vendors identify AI-generated video, no practitioner firsthand testing.
- Comparison of proctoring or browser lockdown capabilities across platforms, minimal discussion of live interview tools.
