The spine — the one argument this article makes

Every section below exists to move this sentence forward. If a section does not serve it, it should not be here.

Every published ranking of video interview software scores workflow, AI analytics and ATS integrations, and none of them rates whether the platform can prove the person on camera is the candidate who applied. This article scores all nine platforms on three integrity axes taken from the vendors' own documentation — ID document check and face match, proctoring and browser lockdown, and detection of deepfaked or proxy candidates in recorded video — then separates the fraud exposure of one-way and live interviewing, and prices the consent, bias-audit and retention duties the vendor's certification does not discharge. The reader is the executive or compliance buyer who signs the contract and answers for the selection process afterwards, and should finish able to shortlist on evidence and defend the shortlist.

The world — what this article is and is not about

Written in the research phase, before any searching started, and never changed since. It is one of the stated reasons the architect benches material below, so read it before judging whether a bench was right.

IS about
Software used by employers to run job interviews over video, and specifically the part of it that verifies the person on camera is the real, consented candidate doing their own work. The reader is a recruiter, talent acquisition lead, hiring manager, or HR/IT buyer at a company that hires remotely and now has to worry about two kinds of candidate fraud: a proxy (a stand-in human taking the interview for someone else, including agency-run interview-as-a-service rings and infiltration schemes like fake IT-worker candidates) and a deepfake (a real-time face swap, synthetic video feed, voice clone, or virtual-camera injection that puts a fabricated or borrowed identity in the interview seat). The subject covers how such fraud actually happens in a hiring interview, how you detect it (liveness and presentation-attack detection, injection and virtual-camera detection, ID document matching against the live face, device and environment signals, browser/screen-share and second-device monitoring, off-camera coaching or AI-answer detection, recorded-vs-live formats, structured questions and follow-up probing that a stand-in fails), what the software can and cannot catch, how these checks slot into the wider hiring workflow alongside skills assessment and proctoring, what a fraud flag should trigger, candidate experience and consent, false positives, and the legal and compliance side of identity checks and biometrics in hiring (consent, data retention, bias, GDPR/BIPA/EU AI Act-type constraints). Buying-side questions belong here too: which vendors do this, what to ask them, what the anti-fraud features are actually called, and how to evaluate whether they work.
is NOT about
This is about video INTERVIEWS FOR HIRING and candidate identity fraud in them. It is NOT about: journalistic, podcast, documentary, or qualitative-research video interviews, nor tools for recording or editing those; not customer or user-research interviews; not video interview practice or coaching products aimed at helping CANDIDATES perform better, nor 'how to pass an interview' advice, nor tools that help a candidate use AI in an interview (we write for the employer detecting that, not the person doing it). Not general video conferencing or webinar software (Zoom, Teams, Meet) reviewed as meeting tools, nor video hosting, video editing, or video marketing/testimonial platforms. DEEPFAKE here means synthetic identity in a live hiring interview only. It is NOT about political misinformation and election deepfakes, celebrity or non-consensual deepfake pornography, deepfake detection for newsrooms, social platforms, or content moderation, deepfake voice scams targeting finance teams (CEO fraud, wire-transfer vishing, invoice fraud), deepfake romance or investment scams, or the general ethics and law of synthetic media outside hiring. PROXY here means a human stand-in impersonating a candidate. It is NOT the networking or software sense (proxy server, HTTP/SOCKS proxy, reverse proxy, VPN setup, proxy pattern in code), NOT proxy voting, proxy statements, or proxy fights in corporate governance, and NOT 'proxy variable' or 'proxy metric' in statistics and research. Identity verification here is HIRING identity verification: not KYC/AML customer onboarding for banks, fintech, crypto exchanges, or marketplaces, not age verification, not gig-worker or driver onboarding checks, not government eID programmes, and not workforce login security (MFA, SSO, passwordless, account takeover, insider threat, or verifying existing employees on internal calls). SCREENING here means candidate screening. It is NOT medical, health, cancer, or genetic screening, not drug testing, and not airport or security screening. Adjacent but out: academic and certification exam proctoring for universities, schools, and test centres (the reader is hiring, not invigilating a degree exam); background checks, criminal record and reference checks, right-to-work and employment verification, credential or degree verification, and resume-lie detection (all about the person's history, not who is on camera right now); and general interview-question banks, scorecards, or interview-training content that never touches fraud. Not a general overview of AI in recruiting, ATS selection, or hiring automation, and not company or product news that only mentions deepfakes in passing.
