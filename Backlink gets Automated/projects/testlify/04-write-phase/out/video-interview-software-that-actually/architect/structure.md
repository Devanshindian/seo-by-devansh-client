# STRUCTURE — Video Interview Software That Actually Stops Deepfake and Proxy Candidates

**Format:** comparison-rankings · **Sections:** 14 · **Authored H3s:** 1 · **Boxes used:** 38

**Options:** HireVue · Testlify · VidCruiter · Willo · Spark Hire · Hireflix · Interviewer.AI · Jobma · Talview
**Yardsticks:** identity verification depth · proctoring and monitoring scope · one-way and live coverage · compliance and data handling · candidate friction · pricing and contract terms

**The article's spine:** Every published ranking of video interview software scores workflow, AI analytics and ATS integrations, and none of them rates whether the platform can prove the person on camera is the candidate who applied. This article scores all nine platforms on three integrity axes taken from the vendors' own documentation — ID document check and face match, proctoring and browser lockdown, and detection of deepfaked or proxy candidates in recorded video — then separates the fraud exposure of one-way and live interviewing, and prices the consent, bias-audit and retention duties the vendor's certification does not discharge. The reader is the executive or compliance buyer who signs the contract and answers for the selection process afterwards, and should finish able to shortlist on evidence and defend the shortlist.

**What it left out, and why:** Three of the four expected topics get their own sections: the selection criteria/methodology block, the at-a-glance comparison table before the write-ups, and the per-vendor sections. The fourth, "what video interviewing software is / introduction to the category", is folded into the criteria section as a two-line category definition rather than given a heading of its own: the intent is commercial, nine option sections are mandatory, and a 2,835-word budget cannot fund a definitional section a buyer at decision stage will skip. I also dropped the SERP's own capability buckets (AI & analytics, ATS integration, candidate experience as feature lists), because scoring communication-analysis features would pull directly against the article's argument that scoring AI is not an identity control. Section count runs to fourteen: nine are the mandatory option sections, three are demanded by the format (criteria block, at-a-glance table, closing best-for verdicts), and two carry the angle's named differentiators (the one-way/live split, and the compliance load).
