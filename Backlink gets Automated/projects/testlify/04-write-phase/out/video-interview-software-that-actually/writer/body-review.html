How We Scored Nine Platforms on Interview Integrity wrote 414 words, was asked for 340 +22% used 15 facts · 7 of them numbers · 8 sources
What this section was asked to do: Fix the scoring standard the rest of the article is judged against. Define what counts as an employer video interview platform, state the fraud premise in numbers with its honest counterweight, separate AI assistance from impersonation so a vendor claim can be read correctly, and name what the published rankings score instead of integrity.
What the writer actually wrote

Every platform in this article is scored on what its own security page, help center, product documentation, and sales material state about candidate identity verification, as of August 2026 [c859]. Marketing category claims do not count. Where a vendor publishes nothing on an axis, the row records a documentation gap rather than a low score, because those are different findings for a buyer.

An employer video interview platform, for the purpose of this list, is one an employer, recruiter, or hiring manager buys, one that collects and scores real candidate interviews, and one priced per month, per interview, or by company size [c452]. General video conferencing is out. It carries no verification, monitoring, or analysis layer at all [c2436].

The threat premise comes from Gartner, which projects that by 2028 one in four candidate profiles worldwide could be fake [c1]. That number moves budgets, so it deserves its counterweight. In Gartner's own survey of 3,000 candidates, 6% admitted to interview fraud, either posing as someone else or having someone else pose as them [c3]. VidCruiter's own material argues that headlines about state operatives overstate how saturated the market really is, and that current deepfakes remain relatively easy to detect visually and with software [c645][c647]. HireVue's research found no score drift in pre-employment assessments between 2022 and now [c648].

The distinction that makes a vendor claim readable is between assistance and impersonation. Assistance means the person on the call is the person who applied, getting help answering questions [c1379]. Impersonation means they are not. Assistance is a process design problem solved by better questions; impersonation is a security problem that identity verification has to catch, and no redesign of the interview removes it [c1297]. Most teams have bought one tool and pointed it at both [c1377]. A policy that conflates them will over-flag honest candidates.

Published rankings score neither. One review process that tested more than 80 video interview tools over four years weighted user-friendliness and integration most heavily, then used employer branding and support quality to separate close calls [c249][c251]. Identity verification appears nowhere in that rubric. Even the compliance criterion recruiters are handed, SOC 2 and GDPR and consent management, is a data-protection claim rather than a proof-of-identity claim [c2352]. Buyer indifference is part of the reason: our own topline report with SHRM Labs found 63% of HR and talent acquisition practitioners do not look for proctoring capability in a provider at all [c8017].

So a vendor scoring on one column and nothing on the others is a partial control, not a solved problem. Even the strongest cell here has a ceiling. A check run before the interview cannot stop a different person from joining the call or AI from feeding answers in real time [c1006].

What the writer actually wrote

VidCruiter takes the top rating because its documentation evidences the widest integrity stack in the set, but its AI Candidate Fraud Detection covers pre-recorded interviews only [c1029]. That single scoping decision is the whole story of this entry.

So a buyer whose final rounds are live gets an audit trail, not an identity gate. VidCruiter's own funnel puts live interviews downstream of pre-recorded shortlisting [c1026], which means the fraud detection stops one stage before the decision does.
