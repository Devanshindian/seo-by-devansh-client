9 Video Interview Platforms, Scored on Deepfake Defense

How We Scored Nine Platforms on Interview Integrity

Every platform in this article is scored on what its own security page, help center, product documentation, and sales material state about candidate identity verification, as of August 2026 1. Marketing category claims do not count. Where a vendor publishes nothing on an axis, the row records a documentation gap rather than a low score, because those are different findings for a buyer.

An employer video interview platform, for the purpose of this list, is one an employer, recruiter, or hiring manager buys, one that collects and scores real candidate interviews, and one priced per month, per interview, or by company size. General video conferencing is out. It carries no verification, monitoring, or analysis layer at all 2.

The threat premise comes from Gartner, which projects that by 2028 one in four candidate profiles worldwide could be fake 3. That number moves budgets, so it deserves its counterweight. In Gartner's own survey of 3,000 candidates, 6% admitted to interview fraud, either posing as someone else or having someone else pose as them 3. VidCruiter's own material argues that headlines about state operatives overstate how saturated the market really is, and that current deepfakes remain relatively easy to detect visually and with software 4. HireVue's research found no score drift in pre-employment assessments between 2022 and now 5.

The distinction that makes a vendor claim readable is between assistance and impersonation. Assistance means the person on the call is the person who applied, getting help answering questions 6. Impersonation means they are not. Assistance is a process design problem solved by better questions; impersonation is a security problem that identity verification has to catch, and no redesign of the interview removes it 6. Most teams have bought one tool and pointed it at both 6. A policy that conflates them will over-flag honest candidates.

Published rankings score neither. One review process that tested more than 80 video interview tools over four years weighted user-friendliness and integration most heavily, then used employer branding and support quality to separate close calls 7. Identity verification appears nowhere in that rubric. Even the compliance criterion recruiters are handed, SOC 2 and GDPR and consent management, is a data-protection claim rather than a proof-of-identity claim 8. Buyer indifference is part of the reason: our own topline report with SHRM Labs found 63% of HR and talent acquisition practitioners do not look for proctoring capability in a provider at all.

Read a row across, not down. No single column is a gate, because each control catches a different attack and fails in a different way. Eye tracking catches gaze-based cheating, browser proctoring catches tab switching and screen sharing, and reasoning checks catch AI-written answers 20. High-stakes programs generally need at least two of those layers running together 20.

So a vendor scoring on one column and nothing on the others is a partial control, not a solved problem. Even the strongest cell here has a ceiling. A check run before the interview cannot stop a different person from joining the call or AI from feeding answers in real time 21.

The gaps matter as much as the entries. No vendor in this set documents liveness or presentation-attack testing to ISO/IEC 30107-3, the standard for proving a camera check rejects a photo, a replayed video, or a mask 1. Jobma's page names liveness detection but attaches no test, level, or third-party provider to it 18. HireVue publishes no ID capture, face match, or deepfake detection at all 14.

Pricing thins out for the same reason integrity claims do. VidCruiter, Jobma, and HireVue all price by quote, so two organizations of the same size can be given two different numbers 16. Integrity features are often the add-on rather than the base plan. The complaint you hear most from buyers is that the module they most need is the one behind a sales call.

VidCruiter: Top-Rated, but Fraud Detection Is One-Way Only

VidCruiter takes the top rating because its documentation evidences the widest integrity stack in the set, but its AI Candidate Fraud Detection covers pre-recorded interviews only 22. That single scoping decision is the whole story of this entry.

The platform runs both live and pre-recorded structured interviews, with rating guides embedded in the live interview interface, and sells video proctoring as a separate priced module alongside skills testing and reference checks 8. Its security posture is the strongest documented here: SOC 2, ISO 27001/17/18, and geo-specific data hosting 7. Pricing starts at $5,000 a year, with per-module costs and one-time setup and training on top 8.

Read the live side carefully. AI Interview Notes produce real-time transcripts, timestamped highlights, and secure auditable recordings 23. Those document the conversation. None of them confirms who is sitting in front of the camera.

So a buyer whose final rounds are live gets an audit trail, not an identity gate. VidCruiter's own funnel puts live interviews downstream of pre-recorded shortlisting 23, which means the fraud detection stops one stage before the decision does.

Testlify: Proctoring Built for Tests, Not Live Calls

Testlify scores well on identity and environment controls, and those controls sit inside proctored assessments rather than a live interview. Its documented identity stack covers face detection, photo ID verification, webcam snapshots, and dual-camera proctoring, with the laptop and mobile cameras giving a continuous view of the room 2425. Environment controls add forced full-screen, a live environment check, and a system requirement check 24. Behavior monitoring covers copy-paste tracking, mouse-out tracking, AI assistance detection, and question-level activity logs 24.

Strictness is configurable in three modes. Standard keeps monitoring light, Strict enforces full-screen and flags a second face or a second monitor, and Custom sets tolerance limits per role 10. Candidates can revoke consent, and the data is wiped within 30 days 25.

The gaps are real. No liveness or presentation-attack certification appears in the published documentation, and nothing here gates identity at a live call.

Talview: Deep Identity Checks, Thin Public Record

Talview documents the most specific identity stack in this comparison after Testlify. Its Digital Identity Check module validates a government-issued ID for tampering, then matches the photo against a selfie or live video feed in real time 26. Its Candidate Verification page adds continuous face matching, voice cross-checking against a verified baseline, and device monitoring that flags an unexpected switch mid-session 27.

The gap is what sits around those checks. Talview's own live interview page pushes the async question to a sales conversation rather than documenting it, so which controls run inside a recorded one-way interview is not on the public record 28. No price appears anywhere: the pricing page is a lead-capture form, with unit-based billing and a minimum commitment of roughly 150-200 candidates as the only stated terms 29. Score the row as a documented gap, not a low mark.

HireVue Scores the Answer but Never Checks the Person

HireVue carries the largest compliance surface and the highest entry price in this set, and nothing in its published material documents ID capture, face match, liveness certification, browser lockdown, or deepfake detection. Pricing starts at roughly $35,000 a year on the Essentials tier 8. Scored video assessment is exactly what the state AI hiring laws were written about 17, so the buyer pays enterprise money for the largest legal exposure and the thinnest identity evidence.

What HireVue does log is real, but it measures the answer. The async product analyzes facial positioning, gaze direction, and speech characteristics, and captures environment images to check the setting 30. The AI scoring itself runs on transcripts, ranking candidates before a recruiter reviews them 31. That establishes how an answer performed, not who gave it.

HireVue CodeVue: the integrity signals sit in the technical assessment, not the video answer

The monitoring that would catch a stand-in lives in the coding challenge. HireVue has described editor playback, paste activity, test runs, inactivity, browser-focus events, and code-similarity signals as technical-review signals 32. Those reconstruct what happened inside the coding session without proving every other application was recorded, and a focus-change event shows the assessment lost focus without revealing the other window 32.

None of it is an identity check. Camera permission, screen sharing on nominated tasks, and code telemetry all describe the session, never the person in front of the camera. For a video-first pipeline, the integrity signals sit in the one stage many roles never reach.

Willo Runs One-Way and Won't Score the Video

Willo runs one-way interviews only, and it refuses on principle to let AI score a video answer. Algorithmic scoring is limited to multiple-choice questions with right or wrong answers, with human reviewers keeping control of the recorded responses 33. The compliance posture behind that is real: ISO 27001 certified, GDPR compliant, data encrypted at rest and primarily hosted in the European Union 3435.

Its RealTalk feature flags answers that look scripted or AI-generated, using a green, amber, and red label on each response 36. Willo calls those signals rather than verdicts, and names strong preparation and non-native speaking styles as expected causes of a flag 36. That is an authenticity check on the speech, not a check on who is speaking.

Willo does sell identity verification, delivered through its API as a right-to-work and background check certified under the UK Digital Identity and Attributes Trust Framework 3738. No published material names a face match against the recorded answer, or any liveness step. Score the row on work eligibility, not on proxy detection.

Interviewer.AI's Face Match Is Sold, Not Included

Interviewer.AI is the only platform here that publishes what its face-match score actually means: 50 or below is LOW, above 50 up to 80 is MEDIUM, and above 80 is HIGH 39. That band is real evidence, and it is more than any rival puts in writing. The catch sits in how you get it. Identity verification is switched on per interview through a flag, not on by default 39. To buy it, you tell the vendor how many candidate assessments you want it enabled for and wait 24 hours for a price 39.

Then there is the product itself. Interviewer.AI ranks candidates automatically, scoring resumes and video on an annual credit allocation 17. Ranking is the whole proposition, not a side feature, which puts the buyer inside automated employment decision rules from the first interview.

Jobma's ID Checks Appear Nowhere in Its Candidate Flow

Jobma documents one usable integrity artifact and no identity gate a buyer can score. The artifact is a per-candidate proctoring report carrying mobile-usage detection and timestamps for when a candidate minimizes the interview screen, filed under a candidate's Proctoring tab and used as a record for comparing competency and integrity 40.

That report describes the room, not the person. Jobma's own candidate walkthrough runs an automatic camera, microphone, and internet speed check, then goes straight to recording 41. No government ID capture, selfie, or face match appears anywhere in it, and the mobile app flow reaches the first question the same way 42. Jobma's marketing claims identity verification in a single clause with no detail on what it checks or when it runs 43.

So the row stays open on the axis that decides this comparison. Environment monitoring is real and evidenced; identity is asserted.

Spark Hire: Scoring That Never Checks Who Recorded It

Spark Hire scores the answer well and never asks who gave it. Its AI Video Review was validated on 68,236 candidate submissions and reaches over 90% accuracy against human ratings, scoring six dimensions including Communication, Execution, and Comprehension 44. Every one of those is a performance signal. None asks whether the person on camera is the applicant.

What is documented is real: one-way and live video, SOC 2 Type II certification, and integrations with more than 40 Applicant Tracking System (ATS) platforms, from $249 a month billed annually 8. The published capability list names no proctoring, browser lockdown, ID document check, or face match 45. The only integrity lever an employer can set is the take limit.

Hireflix: An Invite Link Is Not an Identity Check

Hireflix sits at the floor of this rating because its candidate flow has no account step at all: no application, no log-in, and no registration 7. A link that requires no login binds the response to the link rather than to a verified person, and nothing else in the published material closes that gap. The homepage names no proctoring, no ID document check, and no face matching, and its only security claim is a footer line about SOC 2 46. That certification is Type I, awarded in May 2022, which tests whether controls were designed properly on one date rather than whether they worked over months 47.

The scope is deliberate. Hireflix states it does one-way interviews only and refuses to branch into live interviewing or assessments 46. So buy it where speed is the whole point and the recorded round is a courtesy filter, not a gate: high-volume screening for low-access roles, with identity checked later by someone else.

One-Way vs Live: Where Deepfakes and Proxies Get In

The two formats are not two settings of the same control, and the fraud that is cheap in one is expensive in the other. A one-way interview is a file submitted with no human present. A live interview is a conversation, held in real time through a video platform rather than in person 48. That difference decides which detection signal exists at all, and what a flag can do about it.

The recorded stage is the cheapest place to send a stand-in, because it carries the most candidates and the fewest witnesses. Remote hiring removed the in-person touchpoint older processes kept, and that gap has been noticed and exploited 49. Nothing in the format can ask a follow-up question, so a rehearsed artifact and real understanding look identical on the tape 50. Retakes are allowed, the prompt is fixed, and unmonitored recording is exactly where candidates reach for AI assistance 50.

Live interviewing swaps instrumentation for observation. Tab switching is not a technical flag in a live call, because the interviewer is already on the call; the risk is behavioral rather than technical 51. If the candidate's eyes drift to a second monitor, a person notices, and that holds on any live video call 51. It is a real control, and a fooled human is still a fooled control. Where synthetic video or voice manipulation is the concern, audio and video authenticity review has to sit on top before a candidate advances 52.

So match the control to the stage. Async screening carries the volume, which is where proctoring, lockdown, and identity capture earn their cost. Live rounds carry the judgment, and benefit most for sales, client-facing, and senior leadership hires 8. Most teams run both: async to cut the pool to a shortlist, live for the final conversation 8. The recruiters who catch proxies will tell you the tell usually surfaces in the unscripted follow-up, which only the live format has.

Consent, Bias Audits and Retention Stay With You

A vendor's certification list covers the vendor, not your hiring process. HireVue agreed to a $3.75 million settlement, granted preliminary approval on June 25, 2026, over claims its video interview software extracted facial geometry and voiceprints from Illinois applicants without the notice and consent Illinois' Biometric Information Privacy Act requires 1. The processing at issue is the same facial analysis that would sit behind any face match or liveness check. Buy that capability and you inherit the notice duty with it.

Certification does not move the lawful basis, the candidate notice, or the retention schedule off your desk. About a dozen US states require both parties to consent before a conversation is recorded, so consent has to be taken before the camera starts, not after 53. Privacy duties follow where the candidate lives, not where you are, which is how a single recorded round pulls in the California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA) or the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) at once 53.

Two live cases show where the liability lands. In Mobley v. Workday, the court let discovery proceed on an agency theory, meaning employers may answer for delegating candidate rejection to an automated filter 54. Eightfold AI faces a separate class action under the Fair Credit Reporting Act over hidden profile scores candidates cannot inspect or dispute before rejection 54. Workday disputes all allegations 54.

The bias-audit regimes are named and dated. New York City's Local Law 144 bars using an automated employment decision tool unless an independent bias audit was done within the year, the summary is published, and candidates get 10 business days' notice 55. The employer arranges that audit, not the vendor 56. Under the EU AI Act, hiring AI is high-risk, and the deployer obligations were pushed to December 2, 2027 57. The employer is the deployer, and the vendor's own compliance does not discharge yours 58.

How long each platform keeps the recording

Almost nobody publishes a number. Talview archives every video 90 days after creation, across proctored, live, and async recordings, and retrieval then takes at least two business days through a support ticket 59. HireVue's default runs up to three years, but the actual window is set by the hiring organization rather than by HireVue 60. Jobma and Willo state no retention period at all for recordings, transcripts, or identity records 1815.

That silence is your problem, not theirs. The Age Discrimination in Employment Act (ADEA) requires hiring and recruitment records, including interview notes, to be kept for three years 61. Set the window before you sign, and ask one question first: does the vendor store the ID image, or return only a verdict?

Which Platform to Pick, and When Camera-On Is Enough

Match the verification depth to the access the role carries, not to the size of the budget. Candidates who will touch code, customer data, finance systems, infrastructure, or admin tools need stronger checks than everyone else 52. In a low-access role, a fraudulent hire costs you wasted onboarding and a replacement search 52. In engineering, finance, cybersecurity, or IT administration, the same failure hands credentials, devices, and system access to the wrong person 52.

That difference sets the tier. Basic prevention, meaning a camera-on policy, ID verification, and interviewer training, costs almost nothing beyond the time it takes 2. Platform-level proctoring adds a per-interview cost that scales with volume 2, and biometric verification with AI fraud detection sits at a meaningfully higher price 2, easier to justify for senior roles where a bad hire is expensive 2.

  • Pick VidCruiter when your volume sits in pre-recorded rounds and you want the strongest documented fraud detection in the set, accepting that it does not extend to live interviews.
  • Pick Talview when identity depth is the deciding factor and you can accept commercial terms that are not on the public record.
  • Pick Interviewer.AI when you need a published face-match score and can work with a per-assessment quote.
  • Pick Hireflix or Willo for low-access, high-volume async screening where an invite link and a camera-on rule are proportionate.
  • Pick a proctored assessment ahead of the interview when the risk lives in whether the skill is real, not in who holds the camera.

Most organizations get the best return from layering cheap process controls everywhere and buying technology only for high-stakes interviews 2. Set the strictness per role. One strict default trains your team to ignore the flags.
