# Voices from the field, A Hiring & Assessment Glossary Built for Recruiters, Not HR Generalists

> This is what practitioners said in public discussions, filtered down to what this article can use.
> It is never a fact, never carries a source tag, and never quotes or names a person.

**Coverage.** 6 discussion(s) read and 49 comments, from 3 on linkedin, 3 on reddit. 98 results were looked at across 1 search round(s).

## Vendors get blamed for bad tests, but employers own the legal risk
**What they say.** Practitioners in a legal-risk discussion say the burden of proving a test is valid, especially once it produces adverse impact, sits with the employer, not the assessment vendor. A separate compliance discussion makes the same point about AI-driven screening tools: courts are starting to treat vendor algorithms as legal agents of the employer, so "my vendor's algorithm did that" stops working as a defense.
**Strength.** Moderate. The same claim shows up in two separate discussions on two different platforms, but each thread is thin on its own.
**Serves.** Section 9 (who carries the liability when the tool came from a vendor) and Section 8 (validation as the employer's obligation, not the vendor's).
**What this changes.** Write both entries so the reader understands "validated" and "bias-audited" describe the vendor's tool, but defending it in an audit or a complaint is the employer's job either way.

## A generic competency model can't stand in for a real job analysis
**What they say.** Across a hiring discussion and a recruiting-practice discussion, practitioners converge on the same idea: you cannot pick or defend a test until you have defined what the job actually requires. One thread frames this as "you're not ready to hire for it" without a clear success profile; another explains that off-the-shelf competency models fail because they were never customized to the specific job, so hiring managers don't trust them.
**Strength.** Strong. The point appears in three separate discussions, one of them with six different people independently agreeing.
**Serves.** Section 3 (job analysis as the step that comes before the catalogue).
**What this changes.** State plainly that job analysis is not paperwork before the "real" work starts. It is the reason a generic test catalogue fails and a role-specific one holds up.
