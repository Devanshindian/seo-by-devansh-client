The world — what this article is and is not about

Google returns questions and searches for the WORDS in a query, not for the audience behind them, so some of what comes back belongs to a different field that happens to share our vocabulary. The two lines below are the boundary the vetting used. Read them before judging the keeps and drops.

IS about
The working vocabulary of hiring and candidate assessment: the words, acronyms and metrics that come up when an employer or an agency recruiter sources, screens, tests, interviews, selects and offers a job to a candidate. In scope are sourcing and pipeline terms (Boolean search, talent pool, passive candidate, requisition, ATS, CRM, sourcing channel), screening and selection-method terms (resume screen, phone screen, structured and unstructured interview, work sample, job simulation, cognitive ability test, situational judgement test, personality and behavioural assessment, skills test, coding test, assessment centre, background and reference check, proctoring, one-way video interview), the psychometric and test-design terms recruiters run into when judging whether an assessment is any good (validity, reliability, norms, benchmarking, cut score, adverse impact, test bias, job analysis, KSAs, competency framework, scoring rubric, anchored rating scale), the legal and fairness terms attached to selection decisions (EEOC and the Uniform Guidelines, four-fifths rule, blind or anonymised screening, accommodations, candidate consent and data privacy, AI hiring rules such as NYC Local Law 144 and the EU AI Act), and the numbers recruiters report on (time to hire, time to fill, cost per hire, quality of hire, assessment completion and drop-off rate, offer acceptance rate, funnel conversion, source of hire, candidate experience score). It also covers the shorthand and jargon of the trade (req, hiring manager intake, shortlist, slate, calibration, ghosting, boomerang candidate, silver medallist, TA, RPO). The reader is someone who fills roles for a living, in-house talent acquisition or an agency recruiter, and the terms are explained as they are used in day-to-day recruiting rather than as academic or policy definitions.
is NOT about
Not the general HR or people-ops glossary that starts after someone is hired: payroll, benefits and compensation administration, PTO and leave policy, employee relations, engagement and retention programmes, performance reviews and appraisals, learning and development, succession planning, HRIS and workforce administration, offboarding and exit interviews. Those share the word 'assessment' (performance assessment, training-needs assessment, 360-degree feedback) but describe existing employees, not job applicants. Not the candidate side of the same words: how to pass an assessment, how to write an 'ATS-friendly' resume, practice aptitude tests, or interview prep advice for job seekers. Where a word means something else in another field, this is not about that field. 'Assessment' in education (student testing, formative and summative assessment, exam boards, K-12 and university coursework, LMS quiz tools, SAT/GRE/admissions testing, certification and licensure exams). 'Assessment' in property and finance (tax assessment, property valuation, HOA assessments, credit risk assessment, insurance loss assessment). 'Assessment' in safety, security and compliance (risk assessment, vulnerability assessment, environmental impact assessment, ESG assessment, health and safety audits). 'Assessment' and 'test' in medicine and psychology (clinical or diagnostic assessment, psychiatric evaluation, neuropsychological testing, cancer or health screening, drug and alcohol testing as a medical procedure rather than as a pre-employment check, ATS as the American Thoracic Society). 'Test' in software engineering and QA (unit test, test case, regression testing, test coverage, A/B test). The statistics-textbook senses of validity, reliability, correlation, standard deviation, p-value and sampling, treated as pure statistics rather than as the quality of a hiring assessment. 'Screening' and 'recruitment' in clinical and academic research (recruiting and screening trial participants), in the military (recruiters and enlistment testing such as the ASVAB), in college sports (athletic recruiting, signing day), in politics (candidate as someone standing for election), and in film (a screening). 'Hiring' in the sense of engaging vendors, agencies, contractors or freelancers for a project, and the British sense of hire as renting a car, a venue or equipment. Not a product tour, pricing page or feature comparison of assessment vendors, and not a glossary of generic SaaS, sales or marketing terms that happen to appear in recruiting software.

The winners study

Gaps we chose to own (6)
  • Per-term glossary entries for the hiring vocabulary recruiters actually search (validity, reliability, adverse impact, norming, situational judgment test, work sample, proctoring) — every winner defines the umbrella term once and then stops, so none of the working vocabulary gets its own definition
  • Recruiter-side framing of each term: what it means when you are the one running the assessment, not the one sitting it, which is the exact seat Indeed vacates
  • Legal and fairness terms tied to hiring decisions (adverse impact, EEOC-defensible scoring, accommodation) — absent across all three
  • Anti-cheating and remote-proctoring vocabulary, now central to how assessments actually get run, and unmentioned by any winner
  • Scoping discipline itself: a glossary that stays inside hiring, testing, and pre-employment terms, against Visier's general HR glossary and the workplace-policy padding (dress codes, floating holidays, maternity leave) that dilutes HiPeople's
  • Test-selection logic tied to role and stage, rather than Criteria's flat product list or Indeed's category-level "how employers choose" summary
What every winning article covers (7)
  • What is a skills assessment / skills assessment test (definition)
  • Why companies use skills assessments (the business case)
  • Types of skills assessment tests (hard skills, work sample, cognitive ability, personality)
  • How organizations use them / how employers choose which test to run
  • A named catalogue of specific tests (basic skills, computer literacy, English proficiency, Excel, Word, PowerPoint, typing, ten key)
  • The role of technology / assessment platforms
  • Recommended resources and further reading blocks
Where winners drift off-topic (1)
  • Indeed's page sits in `/career-advice/career-development/` and is written for the candidate taking the test, not the recruiter buying or running one (it pushes an "AI career coach" app and frames the interview as a test the reader will sit); Visier drifts outward into general HR-glossary and workforce-analytics territory plus newsletter CTAs, and Criteria drifts into pure product listing with no definitional coverage at all.
