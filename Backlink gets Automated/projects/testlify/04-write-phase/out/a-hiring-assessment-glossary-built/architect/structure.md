# STRUCTURE — A Hiring & Assessment Glossary Built for Recruiters, Not HR Generalists

**Format:** glossary · **Sections:** 11 · **Authored H3s:** 3 · **Boxes used:** 66


**The article's spine:** A recruiter running screening at volume meets a working vocabulary nobody defines for them from the administrator's seat: validity, reliability, norming, cut score, adverse impact, accommodation, proctoring flags. This article argues that each of those words is an operational setting the recruiter owns, not psychometric background, and defines each one by what it changes in the test they are about to run. Read top to bottom it moves from what a test is and which one to run, through the evidence a vendor owes you, into the numbers you set and the legal exposure attached to them, and ends at the controls you configure on the day. The reader should finish able to choose a test type by role and stage, read a validity claim and a percentile without being sold to, set and defend a cut score, run the four-fifths calculation on their own data, handle an accommodation request, and write a flag review policy before launch.

**What it left out, and why:** All four expected topics are covered. Three get sections of their own: the definition (Section 1), the test types (Section 2), and how employers choose which test to run (Section 3). The fourth, the business case, is folded into Section 1 rather than given its own heading, because at this length a standalone ROI section would repeat the same turnover and cost-per-hire figures the definition section already needs, and the angle earns nothing from it. The format's A-Z hub index is not built as a body section: the article is the hub, its sections are the entries, and there are no separate entry pages to index yet. Google's own four-type answer is covered in Section 2 and then left behind, which is the point of the piece.
