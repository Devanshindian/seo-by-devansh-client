The world — what this article is and is not about

Google returns questions and searches for the WORDS in a query, not for the audience behind them, so some of what comes back belongs to a different field that happens to share our vocabulary. The two lines below are the boundary the vetting used. Read them before judging the keeps and drops.

IS about
Cost per hire as a recruitment budgeting metric: the current public benchmark figure, the formula, and the hard costs (job ads, agency fees, tools) plus soft costs (recruiter and hiring-manager time, onboarding drag) that make it up, broken down by role type and by hiring volume rather than given as one blended average.
is NOT about
This is about the dollar cost of filling one open role and how that cost accounting varies by role and volume. It is NOT about the emotional or burnout toll on recruiters and HR teams (SHRM's wellbeing tangent, not a cost figure), NOT a vendor or product cost-stack comparison like manual vs. AI vs. a specific tool (Metaview's framing), NOT employee compensation or salary benchmarking (what you pay the person once hired, a separate number from what it costs to find them), and NOT the cost of turnover or retention as its own topic — that's a related but distinct HR metric from cost per hire, even though the two get lumped together under 'hiring costs.'

The winners study

Gaps we chose to own (3)
  • No winner segments cost per hire by role/job type (all three keep the breakdown generic — internal vs. external, or leak-by-leak — never by role).
  • No winner segments cost per hire by hiring volume (high-volume vs. low-volume hiring cost structures are absent from every heading list).
  • No winner anchors on a current-year public benchmark figure as a standalone, cited data point — Juicebox's "Benchmarks and average cost" section exists but headings alone don't confirm data recency, so this is the piece's clearest edge over the aging 2022 SHRM figure this page currently cites.
What every winning article covers (7)
  • Definition of cost per hire
  • Formula / how to calculate it
  • Benchmarks and average cost figures
  • Breakdown of what counts as a cost (internal vs. external, hard vs. soft)
  • Factors that drive cost per hire up or down
  • Ways to reduce cost per hire
  • Business impact of high cost per hire (productivity, mis-hires)
Where winners drift off-topic (1)
  • SHRM pivots into recruiter/HR emotional burnout ("The Emotional Toll") — adjacent wellbeing angle, not cost accounting. Metaview frames its entire piece around its own product's diagnostic ("Manual, generic AI, Metaview: the cost stack compared") rather than a neutral benchmark.
